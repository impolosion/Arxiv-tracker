Propose-Filter-Release: Scaling Noise to Typical Sensitivity via Private Filtering
Authors: Moshe Shenfeld, Eliad Tsfadia, Katrina Ligett
First: 2026-10-29T17:59:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:59:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
An estimator can be accurate and stable on typical data, yet sensitive to a few possible records. Differentially private mechanisms commonly scale their noise to the worst-case influence of a single record, which lets these records dictate the noise even when the remaining data would allow far less. Removing them first would restore the smaller scale, but the removal must itself be private. We introduce \emph{propose-filter-release} (PFR), which, by privately filtering its input, turns a mechanism that protects the addition or removal of a typical record, one that fits the remaining data, into one that is differentially private in the standard sense, on every input. Using our novel filtering step, PFR requires only a concave friendliness score, which measures how well a record fits a dataset, and a release mechanism that is friendly-DP, that is, private when the changed record fits the remaining data. For every such score and release mechanism, it guarantees differential privacy under public conditions on the number of records, so it is a general privacy primitive, comparable to propose-test-release, and FriendlyCore can be viewed as one of its implementations. We illustrate the generality of PFR by instantiating it for scalar statistics, principal subspace estimation, the KL divergence of user histograms, the effective resistances of a graph, and treatment-rule selection.

Abstract
Repository-level software engineering (SWE) comprises heterogeneous task categories, whose progress under pooled agentic reinforcement learning can be uneven: gains in some categories coincide with regressions in others, while aggregate resolution obscures these changes. Motivated by this category see-saw, we develop a category-aware expert-training and policy-integration framework. Executable task construction and SWE Labeler, an evidence-grounded multi-axis labeling system, organize the training pools. Initial category-specific RL improves average training success while leaving uneven instance-level progress, motivating explicit consolidation of successful behavior and policy-adaptive task selection. Same-origin category experts alternate long-horizon Agentic-miniRL with Refresh-Repair-Expand (RRE): the updated policy refreshes instance mastery, reuses its own verified successful trajectories for Repair SFT, and reselects tasks for further RL. Label-routed multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) consolidates the experts into one deployable student, with ReLU-gated reward extrapolation keeping only each teacher's improving direction over the reference. Expert training and policy integration require no external model to provide solution trajectories or action targets. On a fixed, audit-filtered Pro-618 subset, we connect Pooled RL and Balanced RL, expert development, and single-model integration through overall and per-category resolution and expert-gain recovery. In final-model evaluations, MOPD achieves mean resolution of 86.19% on SWE-bench Pro V2 and 71.78% on SWE-bench Multilingual, exceeding the base model by 4.57 and 3.89 percentage points, respectively. The same three 27B experts also improve a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B student across the evaluated task sets.

Gym-Anything for Robotics: Scaling physical simulations with digital agents
Authors: Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck
First: 2026-10-29T17:42:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:42:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Realistic, fast robotics simulations could enable large-scale training of embodied AI models that transfer to the real world. However, despite decades of progress, available simulations cover only a narrow range of real-world tasks and physical phenomena. The core bottleneck is that creating better simulations requires significant expert effort and therefore does not scale. To address this, we introduce Gym-Anything for Robotics, a framework in which coding agents create realistic robotics simulations. Our core insight is that simulation creation is a coding agent task, so improvements in coding agents translate directly into richer and more faithful simulations. We select simulations based on U.S. GDP data to cover economically valuable physical work, define what makes a good simulation and a good task, and develop an adversarial creation-audit loop that checks each simulation and task against these definitions. Using this pipeline, we build RoboWorld, a set of 25 simulations with 25 test and 175 training RL environments of varying difficulty. Even the best frontier models fail on every RoboWorld test task, achieving an average reward of only 0.06. Building on RoboWorld, we introduce several new ways for frontier models to improve, including in-context learning across repeated attempts, a privileged teacher that reads the simulator state and teaches a student in context, self-assisted learning from the model's own privileged experience, and code policies that generate demonstrations across varied scenes to train vision-language-action models (VLAs) such as $π_{0.5}$. Each of these substantially improves performance. We release our pipeline, RL environments, and benchmark to facilitate future research. Code and data are available at https://gym-anything.github.io/robotics

Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm
Authors: Yuda Song, Alvin Zhang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora, Jeff Schneider, Daniel Khashabi, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, J. Andrew Bagnell, Aarti Singh, Andrea Zanette
First: 2026-10-29T17:42:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:42:05+00:00
Comments: 67 pages, 31 figures, 22 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Foundation models are typically trained in stages: pretraining, midtraining, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL), with changes in both data and learning algorithms. We show that this algorithmic separation is not fundamental. By deriving suitable RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions, we place all of these stages under a single likelihood-based training algorithm, unified training. This perspective turns the foundation-model training lifecycle into a problem of choosing which data to train on, in what proportions, and when, with conventional staged training as a special case. We instantiate this framework in a unified training system that mixes static data and RL generations within a single trainer. Across controlled and real-world experiments, unified training improves the accuracy-compute tradeoff and task coverage while preserving broader capabilities, and provides a stronger starting point for continual learning. This work lays the groundwork for a unified science of foundation-model training, connecting mathematical principles, training infrastructure, and the development of model capabilities.

Fast and accurate neural triage decoding of ultra-high-rate quantum LDPC codes
Authors: Hong-Ye Hu, Zhenlai Wang, Binzhao Luo, Tao Xiang, Chen Zhao
First: 2026-10-29T17:16:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:16:38+00:00
Comments: 5 pages, 4 figures with Methods. We will release the fully supplementary information in the second update soon
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Quantum error correction is essential for fault-tolerant quantum computing. High-rate quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes have emerged as a promising approach to reducing the substantial physical qubit overhead of quantum error correction, enabling encoding rates exceeding $1/2$ in blocks of approximately one thousand physical data qubits. However, their practical implementation requires decoders that combine high accuracy with low latency to keep pace with quantum hardware. Existing decoders of high-rate qLDPC codes can achieve high accuracy but often incur substantial computational latency, whereas neural network decoders have yet to be demonstrated for high-rate qLDPC codes. Here, we introduce \emph{neural triage decoding}, a framework that uses syndrome consistency to adaptively allocate neural computation. A local neural message-passing network rapidly predicts error corrections, additional iterations reduce residual syndrome violations, and a rarely invoked diversification stage searches for consistent corrections to the remaining shots. We demonstrate that the \texttt{Triage} decoder can decode ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes encoding a few hundred logical qubits, including lifted-product and Kasai codes, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy comparable to the near optimal algorithmic decoders while reducing decoding latency by approximately one to two orders of magnitude. Our framework is inspired by a rigorous analysis of the exact bitwise marginal decoder, revealing a distance-independent syndrome-inconsistent error floor. In addition, we show the robustness of neural network decoders to mismatched error priors. Our results establish adaptive neural decoding as a promising route towards fast, accurate, and scalable quantum error correction in the near term.

Quantum error correction is essential for fault-tolerant quantum computing.
Towards Batch-to-Streaming Deep Reinforcement Learning for Continuous Control
Authors: Riccardo De Monte, Matteo Cederle, Gian Antonio Susto
First: 2026-03-09T16:40:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:11:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
State-of-the-art deep reinforcement learning (RL) methods have achieved remarkable performance in continuous control tasks, yet their computational complexity is often incompatible with the constraints of resource-limited hardware, due to their reliance on replay buffers, batch updates, and target networks. The emerging paradigm of streaming deep RL addresses this limitation through purely online updates, achieving strong empirical performance on standard benchmarks. In this work, we propose two novel streaming deep RL algorithms, Streaming Soft Actor-Critic (S2AC) and Streaming Deterministic Actor-Critic (SDAC), explicitly designed to be compatible with state-of-the-art batch RL methods, making them particularly suitable for on-device finetuning applications such as Sim2Real transfer. Both algorithms achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art streaming baselines on standard benchmarks without requiring tedious per-environment hyperparameter tuning. We further investigate the batch-to-streaming transition, showing that a naive transition does not guarantee preservation of pre-trained policy performance, and propose a principled approach to address this challenge.

Reinforcement learning for control of partial differential equations
Authors: Nicolò Botteghi, Matteo Tomasetto, Andrea Manzoni
First: 2026-10-29T16:59:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:59:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Controlling dynamical systems governed by partial differential equations (PDEs) remains a challenging task in scientific computing, owing to high-dimensional state spaces, distributed actuation, nonlinear dynamics, and stringent real-time constraints. Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for synthesizing feedback controllers that adapt to varying operating conditions and can be deployed efficiently in real-time. This review provides a unified perspective on RL and PDE-constrained optimization, and surveys model-free, model-based, and multi-agent RL approaches to PDE control, with particular emphasis on sample efficiency, generalization across parametric variations, and scalability to high-dimensional problems. Two demonstrative examples, the stabilization of a chaotic system and a flow control problem, illustrate the performance and pitfalls of RL agents in practice. Finally, we discuss evaluation and benchmarking practices, current limitations, and open research directions toward reliable, data-efficient, and scalable RL for scientific computing.

Framework-Conditional Open Materials Generation
Authors: Maya M. Martirossyan, Hillary Pan, Philipp Höllmer, Stefano Martiniani
First: 2026-10-29T16:57:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:57:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Theoretical predictions of novel materials rely on a variety of techniques, from data-mining-based ion substitution methods to fully de novo generative models. Each approach imposes specific constraints and encounters unique challenges when extrapolating beyond the distribution of known materials to discover new ones. The foremost goal of materials generation is to produce stable, unique, and novel (S.U.N.) structures. Predicted structures that meet these criteria, however, typically result from novel chemical compositions mapped onto known structural prototypes or frameworks. We leverage this heuristic insight to present a new generative paradigm for the de novo prediction of inorganic crystalline materials constrained by their structural framework---i.e., the unit cell shapes and respective particle positions. Our method, termed OMatG-FC (Open Materials Framework-Conditional Generation), learns via a combination of discrete flow matching on the atomic species and stochastic interpolants for the volume scaling of the unit cell lattice, while fixing in-place the fractional coordinates and the unit-volume lattice vectors, dramatically reducing the dimensionality of the problem compared to unconstrained (framework-free) generation. Our method is more flexible than existing prototype-constrained methods, demonstrating capability for discovery of unseen, stable prototypes derived from known structural frameworks. We also investigate the number of unique frameworks in the MP-20 dataset, demonstrating the propensity of duplicate frameworks within the dataset and providing a framework-split version. We benchmark the performance of our model using the LeMat-GenBench suite using three ML interatomic potentials and achieve state-of-the-art S.U.N. rate below the convex hull, eschewing the need to learn atomic packings for materials discovery.

Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis: integrating reinforcement learning and conformal prediction for reliable wafer defect classification
Authors: Aleksandr Dekhovich, Carlas Smith, Oleg Soloviev

Abstract
Semiconductor manufacturing is a complex, multi-step process that requires both high precision and low operating costs. Therefore, detecting defects in a high-volume setting is an important component of wafer map analysis for reliable production. However, most state-of-the-art deep learning approaches require a full scan of the surface, which hinders the development of high-throughput inspection systems. To address this problem, we propose Uncertainty-aware Feedback Loop Diagnosis (UFLD), a novel algorithm that employs reinforcement learning for adaptive measurement acquisition and provides a prediction uncertainty level after each step, stopping the process once sufficient information is obtained. Unlike existing methods that rely on ensemble models or auxiliary confidence estimation deep learning models, UFLD utilizes a more parameter-efficient conformal prediction framework to determine the optimal stopping time. The proposed UFLD reduces acquisition time by about $40\%$ and achieves $98.4\%$ inspection accuracy, without requiring an additional learned confidence-estimation network. We validated our approach on the MixedWM38 dataset, demonstrating its advantages over existing adaptive acquisition procedures and standard convolutional neural networks.

SCARL: Sequence Confidence-Aligned Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Zhenghong Ke, Jingbo Zhou, Hua Wu
First: 2026-10-29T16:38:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:38:11+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) improves language-model reasoning by scoring sampled responses with a rule-based verifier and reinforcing those that pass. Where a classifier's training couples predicted probability to labels, nothing ties an RLVR policy's confidence in a response to its reward: aligned training would drive responses with the same advantage toward equal confidence, yet the advantage is blind to confidence. Under this sequence-confidence misalignment, a success the policy barely found earns the same update as one it produces with near certainty, and a confident error is corrected no harder than an exploratory one. We propose SCARL (Sequence Confidence-Aligned Reinforcement Learning), which reshapes, but never re-signs, each advantage with the alignment gap between the response's group-relative confidence and the outcome its advantage certifies, normalized into shares of each class's advantage budget. Misaligned samples absorb the force, responses with the same advantage are driven toward equal confidence, and repairing the misalignment doubles as a remedy for the entropy collapse that erodes exploration. SCARL provably reduces to its base estimator when a class's confidences agree, adds no extra passes and one hyperparameter. On Qwen3-4B-Base it lifts average Pass@32 by 2.4 points over DAPO and is best among nine compute-matched methods on both coverage and per-draw accuracy; applied unchanged across four estimators and four backbones, it improves 14 of 16 settings while preserving policy entropy and widening the confidence-correctness separation. Our code is available at https://github.com/kezhh163/scarl.

Continuous-Action World Models: Learning Cross-Embodiment Spatiotemporal Dynamics Priors

Abstract
We introduce Continuous-Action World Models (CoAWM), a framework for learning cross-embodiment spatiotemporal dynamics priors that support robot action generation across embodiments and execution frequencies. At its core is a shared latent function space that captures the spatial and temporal structure of robot actions. We learn this space through two stages. First, function pretraining brings trajectories from different embodiments and sampling frequencies into a common representation, learning shared dynamics priors through action reconstruction and consistency across temporal sampling views. By accommodating heterogeneous robot trajectories, this stage broadens the range of data sources available for pretraining and facilitates scaling to larger and more diverse datasets. Second, we connect the pretrained function space to a world-model backbone, enabling observations, language instructions, and the current robot state to condition the prediction of a latent action function. A single decoder takes any query time and returns the corresponding end-effector action. By separating shared dynamics priors from task-conditioned action selection and temporal sampling, our approach provides a unified foundation for transferring robot experience across embodiments and control frequencies.

Parallelizable memory recurrent units
Authors: Florent De Geeter, Gaspard Lambrechts, Damien Ernst, Guillaume Drion
First: 2026-01-14T14:01:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:26:01+00:00
Comments: This work has been the subject of patent applications (Numbers: EP26151077 and EP26175248.9)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
With the emergence of massively parallel processing units, parallelization has become a desirable property for new sequence models. The ability to parallelize the processing of sequences with respect to the sequence length during training is one of the main factors behind the uprising of the Transformer architecture. However, Transformers lack efficiency at sequence generation, as they need to reprocess all past timesteps at every generation step. Recently, state-space models (SSMs) emerged as a more efficient alternative. These new kinds of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) keep the efficient update of the RNNs while gaining parallelization by getting rid of nonlinear dynamics (or recurrence). SSMs can reach state-of-the-art performance through the efficient training of potentially very large networks, but still suffer from limited representation capabilities. In particular, SSMs cannot exhibit persistent memory, or the capacity of retaining information for an infinite duration, because of their monostability. In this paper, we introduce a new family of RNNs, the memory recurrent units (MRUs), that combine the persistent memory capabilities of nonlinear RNNs with the parallelizable computations of SSMs. These units leverage multistability as a source of persistent memory, while getting rid of transient dynamics for efficient computations. We then derive a specific implementation as proof-of-concept: the bistable memory recurrent unit (BMRU). This new RNN is compatible with the parallel scan algorithm. We show that BMRU achieves good results in tasks with long-term dependencies, and can be combined with SSMs to create parallelizable hybrid networks that have both transient dynamics and persistent memory.
Summary / 总结
With the emergence of massively parallel processing units, parallelization has become a desirable property for new sequence models.

Abstract
Unsupervised environment design (UED) methods such as PLR, ACCEL, SFL and PAIRED are compared mostly on 2D mazes, grid worlds and BipedalWalker, while terrain curricula for legged robots are hand-designed and evaluated against a few baselines. Abstract
Scaling critic capacity is a promising direction for improving off-policy reinforcement learning (RL). However, recent work shows that larger critics are prone to overfitting and instability in replay-based bootstrapped training. In this paper, we propose using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) as a structural regularizer for critic learning from scratch. Our approach freezes randomly initialized base matrices and optimizes only the corresponding low-rank adapters, thereby constraining the learned weight changes to low-rank residuals. We evaluate our method on state-based continuous-control tasks, using SAC with SimbaV2 and BRC on DMC-Hard, and FastTD3 with its SimbaV2 variant on IsaacLab. Empirically, LoRA reduces critic loss during training and improves overall policy performance, achieving the best or competitive results on most tasks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our low-rank updates provide a simple and effective form of structural regularization for critic learning in off-policy RL.

Rimuru: Unified Memory Management for Modern LLM Serving via Elastic Expert Offloading
Authors: Zhiyuan Fang, Dingyang Li, Jianfeng Zhong, Bin Wang, Weixi Zhu, Cunshu Ni, Zicong Hong, Wuhui Chen, Zibin Zheng
First: 2026-10-29T15:47:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T15:47:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Modern LLMs strain device memory through large model weights and attention states, particularly in resource-constrained scenarios. Existing systems separately provision memory for model weights and attention states, imposing fixed fixed boundaries that cannot adapt to the changing memory demands of workloads. Under heavy workloads, insufficient state capacity limits request concurrency and reduces throughput; under light workloads, idle memory in one region cannot be reallocated to another, wasting device memory. Meanwhile, modern LLM architectures introduce heterogeneous tensors with diverse layouts and lifetimes, further complicating memory management. These problems motivate unified memory management for heterogeneous tensors. However, a shared pool alone does not make memory directly reusable across tensors with different allocation granularities and execution layouts. This paper presents Rimuru, a modern LLM serving system that unifies memory management through elastic expert offloading. Rimuru establishes a shared page pool to enables elastic memory sharing across heterogeneous tensors. To accommodate different allocation granularities, Rimuru introduces memory views that allow each tensor type to interpret the shared memory space using its own page size and page table. Furthermore, Rimuru combines huge-page alignment with a multi-level page allocator that splits and merges pages, enabling cross-type capacity reuse. A page-table-aware fused MoE kernel directly accesses non-contiguous expert weights, avoiding copy overhead. Building on these mechanisms, Rimuru employs a workload-aware adjuster that dynamically reallocates pages across tensor types according to runtime demands. Implemented on top of vLLM, Rimuru remains compatible with modern LLMs. Evaluations show that Rimuru achieves up to 19.03x baseline throughput and reduces TTFT by 98.1% on average.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning with general utilities optimizes nonlinear functionals of policy-induced occupancy measures. We develop a natural actor-critic method for concave RLGU objectives using an exact variational dual representation, whose existence follows from concavity and differentiability, with explicit constructions for a broad class of separable occupancy utilities. Rather than estimating the occupancy measure and then forming the policy-gradient pseudo-reward, our method learns this pseudo-reward directly as a function of the state-action pair using a neural critic. The critic is trained by projected stochastic updates, while multilevel Monte Carlo combines outputs across training horizons to control its bias at logarithmic expected cost. Our analysis separates conditional actor bias from estimator second moments and accounts explicitly for dual optimization, critic approximation, and neural linearization errors. Under policy-transfer, dual-stability, and local critic-regularity conditions, the method achieves $\widetilde{O}(T^{-1/2})$ global convergence above policy, critic-class, and finite-width approximation errors using $\widetilde{O}(T)$ expected environment transitions and reference samples. For a fixed critic representation, this yields $\widetilde{O}(ε^{-2})$ sampling complexity above the approximation floor, covering infinite state-action spaces and occupancy utilities beyond fixed finite vectors of returns.

Abstract
Small-molecule identification from tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) may require complementary measurements. Herein, we propose a structure-informed deep Q-network (SI-DQN) that uses existing MS/MS evidence to decide whether further characterization is needed, which measurement to request, and when to stop. The policy combines spectral representations and candidate rankings with score-weighted descriptors of the candidates' chemical features and structural differences. The policy was evaluated on 79,462 held-out samples with simulated MS/MS, infrared (IR), and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra from the Multimodal Spectroscopic Dataset. Evaluation used fixed hard candidate sets containing the correct structure and equal normalized acquisition costs. Averaged across three training runs, SI-DQN increased Top-1 accuracy from 80.10% to 93.78% for positive-ion MS/MS starts and from 70.44% to 93.31% for negative-ion starts. The corresponding mean additional measurement counts were only 0.227 and 0.340 per sample, respectively. Complete acquisition achieved 97.72% accuracy with four additions, whereas SI-DQN reduced the additional measurement count by 94.3% and 91.5%. Relative to a rule-based policy, SI-DQN reduced the composite loss combining terminal ranking error and acquisition cost by 19.3% and 25.5%. Adding structural information to DQN further improved accuracy while reducing requests across all three training runs. At comparable ranking uncertainty, SI-DQN increased request rates relative to DQN more strongly in groups with high structural disagreement, where larger reductions in composite loss were also observed. Together, these results support using the ambiguity among candidate structures after MS/MS analysis to guide further characterization, providing a quantitative basis for balancing identification performance against subsequent measurement effort.

Abstract
Autonomous highway driving for heavy-duty trucks is a challenging control problem. The greater mass, dimensions and intricate dynamics increase the requirements for cooperation from surrounding traffic in critical maneuvers, requiring a careful balance between safety constraints and efficient long-horizon decision making. Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides rigorous constraint satisfaction but relies on terminal cost which can limit its effectiveness at short, computationally affordable horizons. Reinforcement Learning (RL) captures long-term driving behavior but offers no constraint guarantees and tends toward overly conservative policies when trained under sparse collision penalties. In this paper, we propose a hybrid MPC and RL framework for highway driving in autonomous trucks, where a finite-horizon MPC, solved via dynamic programming, uses a pre-trained RL model for terminal cost estimation. We evaluate the framework in a simulation environment with reactive surrounding traffic and compare it against pure MPC with zero terminal cost, rollout with terminal cost estimation using a base policy, and pure RL. Results show that the hybrid approach achieves zero collisions with the lowest cost of any configuration.

Abstract
Robotic navigation typically assumes a collision-free route to the goal exists; in cluttered environments with movable obstacles, however, reaching the goal may require physical interaction. Classical solutions encode these behaviors through geometric rules and task-specific logic, while learning-based approaches often constrain the decision space to low-dimensional motion commands or predefined action sets. Evaluation Metrics for Safe Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Lindsay Spoor, Aske Plaat, Thomas Moerland
First: 2026-09-14T10:04:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T15:11:46+00:00

Abstract
Safe reinforcement learning (RL) is commonly formalized as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), in which an agent maximizes expected reward while keeping its expected cumulative cost below a specified safety bound. Existing safe RL benchmarks predominantly report whether an algorithm is safe on average, following this expectation-based guarantee. We argue that this convention is insufficient to reliably characterize an algorithm's true safety: it fails to capture how often and how severely the safety bound is violated, whether this holds consistently across tasks and safety bounds, and whether training-time behavior is representative of behavior of the final converged policy. Therefore, we introduce (i) evaluation metrics for safe RL that address each of these concerns and in addition allow for aggregation across tasks and safety bounds. We furthermore define (ii) a safety tier system to systematically categorize and compare algorithms in terms of safety and reliability at both training and for a final policy. Using this framework, we provide (iii) an empirical safety evaluation across multiple safety navigation tasks. Our results show that aggregate metrics, distributional reporting, and task- and safety bound-specific results each reveal information the other metrics cannot. We therefore recommend reporting all three jointly, rather than compressing this information into a single value, as is common practice. We provide SafeRLEval, an open-source evaluation suite to support the reliable characterization of safety in future safe RL research.

Abstract
Artificial intelligence (AI)-based channel state information (CSI) feedback reduces overhead in frequency division duplexing (FDD) massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems, yet its reconstructed CSI depends on the integrity of pilot acquisition. This paper identifies and evaluates the attack path of adversarial pilot injection against this feedback. A transmitted disturbance can damage the user-side estimation before encoding; correctly delivered quantized bits can still represent a contaminated channel estimate. We model this path through attacker propagation, linear minimum mean square error estimation, compression, quantization, and decoding under a total transmit-energy constraint. An adversarial perturbation generator (APG) implements the attack using channel statistics without current legitimate CSI or instantaneous attacker-channel observations at deployment. At a Rician attacker-channel factor of 10~dB and a transmit perturbation-to-signal ratio of $-4$~dB, APG produces a normalized mean-square error (NMSE) of 0.84~dB in base-station CSI reconstruction, compared with $-10.31$~dB for Gaussian interference. Estimator-targeted and reconstruction-targeted attacks yield different error orderings, and reconstruction damage also degrades beamforming. As a countermeasure, alternating adversarial training (AAT) adapts the feedback encoder and decoder to contaminated pilots. With condition-matched training and APGs reoptimized for the final receiver, AAT reduces NMSE by 2.40--3.79~dB across six representative channel--power conditions. The results demonstrate the importance of protecting pilot acquisition alongside quantized feedback delivery.

Abstract
We consider the problem of dynamically selecting among competing strategies using observable predictors. To mitigate estimation noise and excessive switching, we regularise the estimated policy with a switching cost, which may exceed actual transaction costs when these are too small to provide sufficient regularisation. Since switching costs make current choices affect future decisions, we formulate the problem using dynamic programming. Abstract

Abstract
Offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning remains challenging in stochastic long-horizon settings, where compounding value estimation errors hinder reliable goal reaching. While prior methods have sought to address this challenge through various approaches, a key challenge remains path mirage, where agents overcommit to spuriously successful trajectories in offline datasets induced by stochasticity, often leading to failure on difficult tasks. To address this issue, we propose Branch-aware Graph Planning (BGP), which captures stochastic branching structures by identifying high-variability points as graph nodes and designing goal-conditioned edges with segmented learning to avoid unreliable branches and reduce unnecessary stochasticity along paths. As a result, BGP enables reliable goal reaching even in highly stochastic long-horizon environments, and experiments on diverse OGBench tasks show that it substantially outperforms prior state-of-the-art offline goal-conditioned RL methods. Our implementation is available at https://umlic.github.io/papers/BGP/.

Abstract
Children expertly construct mental models under uncertainty despite limited world knowledge. Which computational principles make this possible? We study this question in an interactive physical Box Task in which participants infer a latent cause through interaction with a stochastic and partially observed environment. The Box Task was originally designed to study children's puzzle-solving qualitatively. We contribute a computational analysis of behavior that formalizes the cognitive process by which children generate new hypotheses and respond to environmental uncertainty as inference unfolds. Our modeling framework is based on Bayesian particle-based inference, which maintains a small set of working hypotheses and continually generates new ones. We give two complementary implementations of hypothesis representations within this framework: (1) as constraints on evidence, and (2) as executable programs evaluated against evidence. Using the constraint-based formulation, we show that the model needs to account both for subjective reliability of evidence and online hypothesis generation to explain key signatures of children's behavior. Using the programmatic formulation, we show that hypotheses may take the form of partial compositional rules to guide information gathering when evidence interpretation is hard. The programmatic hypotheses are implemented using LLM-based program synthesis, evaluated across three prompt variants and several LLM backends, demonstrating robustness of our approach. Together, these results extend current models of children's inference to account for subjective reliability of evidence and open-ended hypothesis spaces, and provide a computational analysis of the form generated hypotheses may take in practice.

Abstract
Guidance methods are essential tools for steering the generation process in diffusion models, improving the generalizability of stochastic robotic controllers and enabling high-quality controlled image synthesis. Despite strong parallels between flow-matching and diffusion, integrating guidance into flow-matching models remains quite challenging. Recent work has proposed general guidance frameworks for flow-matching, however, they are direct adaptations of diffusion guidance methods and can prove unstable. In this work, we conduct a theoretical analysis of guidance in flow-matching and show that a naive translation of diffusion-based guidance to flow-matching breaks the coupling distribution used during training, resulting in weaker control over sampling accuracy. Our findings show that our framework improves sample quality and guidance stability across all tasks, without imposing any additional overhead.
