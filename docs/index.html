Joints, Camera, Action! Co-Generating 3D Motion and Video for Arbitrary Rigs
Authors: Noam Rotstein, Yoad Tewel, Chen Tessler, Haggai Maron, Gal Chechik, Or Litany, Yoni Kasten, Yuval Atzmon
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Video generation models can now depict remarkably realistic motion. Yet it is unclear how to use this motion beyond pixels: to animate a specific 3D asset and replay the animation in its 3D scene. For instance, a video model can show a monkey jumping over a crossbar, but it cannot return that jump as replayable 3D motion placed in the 3D scene. Here we present a model that directly co-generates the video and 3D motion for any supplied rig, along with a camera that registers this motion to the scene. Prior video-motion models either handle only human skeletons, often adding billions of parameters, or triangulate 3D motion from generated 2D pose maps. We instead keep 3D motion in a separate model that supports arbitrary skeletons, and connect it to a pretrained video model through a small set of cross-attention layers. This separation lets the motion model first learn how skeletons move from motion-only data, which is easier to obtain and augment than paired video. Additionally, our model learns to locate each joint in the video frames, which lets us recover the camera and place the 3D motion in the scene. To train across many skeletons, we use a generative video editor to place animated 3D assets in scenes suited to their actions, curating the edits so their known motion remains valid. Together with human and robot video, this yields nearly 200K training pairs. Our evaluations demonstrate that our model animates diverse rigs from text more faithfully than video-first pipelines, and its motion responds to the surrounding scene. It also turns video into rig motion, rig motion into video, and enables keyframe-based control of the 3D motion. Videos and additional results are available on our project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/.

Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking
Authors: Yuxuan Kuang, Jiashun Wang, Yixuan Sun, Yufei Ye, Sungjae Park, Umar Iqbal, Katerina Fragkiadaki, Shubham Tulsiani
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:32+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. Goal-reaching reinforcement learning generalizes to arbitrary object poses but, lacking any human prior, converges to unnatural and unreliable finger strategies; motion tracking reproduces human-like behavior from a single clip but cannot deviate from it. We present AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that turns a single hand-object trajectory of a few seconds---from mocap data or HOI reconstruction from in-the-wild RGB(D) video---into an adaptable dexterous skill. At the core of AdaDex is Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking: a shared policy is trained on two populations of massively parallel environments: motion tracking environments trained with dense motion tracking objective, which instill the human prior from human motion; and generalization environments with noised initial states and goals and a sparse object-success reward, which force the skill to work far off the reference. From seconds of human motion per skill, the resulting policies can learn diverse adaptable dexterous skills, such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch. Our method outperforms baselines in terms of generalization and functionality, and we demonstrate transfer across simulators and to the real world. Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/.

Efficient Edge-aware Attention Network for Graph Generation

Abstract
Graph generative models commonly use graph-specific Transformer architectures to jointly update node and edge features. However, these architectures do not directly benefit from recent hardware-aware attention implementations, limiting their scalability. The adaptation is, however, non-trivial: edge and graph-level features must be injected into pairwise attention scores, graph masks must be respected, and dynamic edge updates remain a major source of quadratic memory usage. We propose an edge-aware attention layer for graph generation that incorporates structural information through head-wise score modulation, while updating edge representations with a lightweight residual mechanism that retains attention-score dependent edge updates. This layer can be effectively implemented using PyTorch's FlexAttention. We achieve similar generation quality to our baseline while reducing peak GPU memory by around 65% and substantially improving training and sampling throughput. These results suggest that the proposed architecture is a practical step toward scalable graph foundation models.

Entanglement Distribution in Quantum Switch Networks: Memory Limits and Achievability
Authors: Lei Hu, Alptug Aytekin, Stavros Mitrolaris, Mohamed Nomeir, Subhankar Banerjee, Sennur Ulukus, Saikat Guha
First: 2026-10-20T17:46:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:46:05+00:00
Comments: The abstract was shortened to meet arXiv's character limit
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study the minimum persistent memory required to distribute a graph state through a quantum switch. Specifically, consider a central switch that assists $n$ remote users in distributing a graph state, using independently generated user-switch Bell pairs. Since Bell-pair generation is probabilistic, different links generally succeed at different times. When a Bell-pair is generated, the qubits may need to be stored in memory while waiting for other links to succeed. Recently, new protocols have been proposed to reduce this storage overhead by processing and releasing some switch qubits before all Bell links are available. However, these protocols do not characterize the minimum persistent switch memory required at each stage. Moreover, the local-cover method in the state-of-the-art Piecemaker protocol is computationally expensive for general target graphs. In this work, we characterize the minimum persistent switch memory required after any subset of users' Bell links are generated and processed. We then develop an online stabilizer-transition protocol that attains this limit, which can be implemented in polynomial time in the number of qubits $n$. Remarkably, this optimal protocol can provide substantial memory reductions over existing heuristic approaches. In particular, for the path graph $P_n$ over $n$ users, Piecemaker protocol requires at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ qubits of peak persistent memory for \textit{every Bell-links generation order}, whereas under the order $(1,2,\ldots,n)$, our protocol requires only one persistent switch qubit. Finally, the same stabilizer-transition framework also provides a direct forward construction for sequential graph-state generation using the minimum number of quantum emitters, equal to the maximum of the height function.

Abstract
Developing visual perception models for active agents and sensorimotor control are cumbersome to be done in the physical world, as existing algorithms are too slow to efficiently learn in real-time and robots are fragile and costly. This has given rise to learning-in-simulation which consequently casts a question on whether the results transfer to real-world. In this paper, we are concerned with the problem of developing real-world perception for active agents, propose Gibson Virtual Environment for this purpose, and showcase sample perceptual tasks learned therein. Gibson is based on virtualizing real spaces, rather than using artificially designed ones, and currently includes over 1400 floor spaces from 572 full buildings. The main characteristics of Gibson are: I. being from the real-world and reflecting its semantic complexity, II. having an internal synthesis mechanism, "Goggles", enabling deploying the trained models in real-world without needing further domain adaptation, III. embodiment of agents and making them subject to constraints of physics and space.

Continuous-Time Policy Gradient from Discrete Data: Convergence Analysis and Model-Free Algorithms
Authors: Martin Hernandez, Xinyan Qing, Mo Zhou, Yuhua Zhu
First: 2026-10-20T17:33:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:33:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Continuous-time reinforcement learning seeks an optimal policy under unknown dynamics driven by stochastic differential equations using observations collected at discrete times. We develop a policy gradient framework that combines rigorous convergence analysis with learning from discrete data. We first establish a performance difference lemma and derive policy gradient formulas for randomized and parametrized policies. We then prove exponential convergence of an entropy-regularized policy flow to the regularized optimum. For discrete parametrized updates, we derive a convergence bound that explicitly separates the contributions of critic error, policy-regression error, and the learning step size. To enable learning under unknown dynamics, we integrate this framework with PhiBE, which constructs a continuous-time surrogate generator from discrete transition data. The resulting on-policy and off-policy model-free actor-critic algorithms preserve the continuous-time structure of the control problem. Numerical experiments on an entropy-regularized linear-quadratic regulator and a nonlinear wheeled robot demonstrate the performance and stability of the proposed methods with both known and data-driven dynamics.

Multi-Marginal Inverse Optimal Transport for Contrastive Learning Via Explicit Anchor-Positive-Negative Coupling
Authors: Ngoc-Hai Nguyen, Thuan Nguyen, Prakash Ishwar, Shuchin Aeron
First: 2026-09-27T16:41:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:59:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Inverse Optimal Transport (OT) based methods for representation learning learn representations such that the global OT coupling between a pair of data marginals in the representation space, concentrates on the positive pairs. This is in contrast to previous methods that primarily focused on pairwise matching. However, these methods $\textit{DO NOT}$ utilize negative pairs and hence are not truly contrastive in their approach. We show that this leads to issues of dimensional collapse and hence degraded downstream performance. To alleviate this, we develop a novel multi-marginal (MM) inverse OT (IOT) contrastive learning (CL) approach called Neg-MMIOT-CL, which learns representations such that the global multi-marginal OT (MMOT) coupling between a triple of data marginals, with respect to a carefully designed ground-cost between triplets of data points in the representation space, concentrates on the anchor-positive-negative $\textit{triplets}$. For a latent class model, we empirically show that Neg-MMIOT-CL alleviates dimensional collapse. Furthermore, for a specific choice of ground cost for all triplets in representation space, we prove that the optimal representation configuration for Neg-MMIOT-CL exhibits equiangular property for within-class and across-class representations, which translates to Neural-Collapse when the representation dimension is larger than the number of classes minus one -- a result that is $\textit{previously established only}$ for pairwise contrastive learning methods. Finally, we propose Neg-IOT-CL-PushPull, that is a computationally efficient alternative to Neg-MMIOT-CL, alleviating the high cost of computing MMOT plans needed during implementation. We apply these methods on both synthetic and real-world datasets and show significant improvements over existing OT-based contrastive learning methods.

Inverse Optimal Transport (OT) based methods for representation learning learn representations such that the global OT coupling between a pair of data marginals in the representation space, concentrates on the positive pairs.

Authors: Kartik Tandon, Alessio Borgi, Pavel Snopov, Inés García-Redondo, Davide D'Ascenzo, Sebastiano Cultrera di Montesano, Fabrizio Silvestri, Pietro Liò, Francesca Dominici, Randall Balestriero, Claudio Battiloro

Abstract
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) offer an appealing approach to self-supervised learning, but are typically prone to representation collapse. LeJEPA resolves this by introducing a theoretically grounded distribution-matching objective that drives the embedding towards an isotropic Gaussian. However, this approach is fundamentally tied to Euclidean assumptions, and its global target does not reflect domain-specific geometric priors, such as the use of non-Euclidean embedding geometries, e.g., equivariant CNNs or hyperbolic ViTs. We thus introduce LocaLeJEPA, a framework motivated by a manifold-theoretic reformulation of LeJEPA's entropy-maximization property. The accompanying LocaLeReg, a novel locally Euclidean distribution-matching regularizer, enables a stable and scalable implementation of the LocaLeJEPA framework. We then perform extensive experiments in the vision setting on models trained on ImageNet-1K. LocaLeJEPA significantly outperforms LeJEPA when paired with hyperbolic ViT-S/8 and equivariant ResNet-50 backbones, and also with ordinary Euclidean ViT-S/8 and ViT-L/14 backbones. This holds both in terms of linear and k-NN classification performance on ImageNet and on a series of downstream tasks tested. We view the LocaLeJEPA formalism as a principled approach to the JEPA paradigm that better respects intrinsic data geometry while also accommodating generalizations to exotic embedding geometries. Confessions of a Masked MDP
Authors: Sebastian Reboul, Lorenzo Croissant, Hélène Halconruy
First: 2026-10-20T15:22:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T15:22:31+00:00
Comments: 18 pages, 7 figures Submitted to AISTATS 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In reinforcement learning, one presumes the action set to be known ahead of time, and, thus, one must know if any state-action pairs are not permissible ahead of computations. In some applications, determining this action mask is the main computational bottleneck. Isolating this problem to highlight its core properties, we study how to combine the search for an optimal policy with the learning of the mask itself, under a linear MDP model in which each attempted action reveals the exact value of a linear score determining its validity. In this framework, we first construct an algorithm based on deterministic optimistic optimization and show that it is minimax optimal, which we show is also computationally efficient. However, it is sub-optimal on non-degenerate instances, as we show. We propose an alternative, which is also computationally efficient, which plans its exploration strategy. This algorithm admits a formal interpretation as an analogue of information-directed-sampling for deterministic feedback, which may be of independent interest. Finally, we study an application of this framework to transfer learning, where the invalid action set changes over time.

Local Work, Global Progress: A Convergence Theory of AI Agent Collaboration
Authors: Ivan Ilin, Peter Richtárik
First: 2026-10-20T14:37:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T14:37:06+00:00
Comments: 59 pages, 15 figures, 28 tables. Code: https://github.com/vectozavr/local-work-global-progress
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Teams of large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly applied to complex tasks, but their collaboration protocols are mostly designed empirically, without convergence guarantees. We formalize collaborative problem solving as block optimization: each agent improves one part of a shared solution, guided by its local objective plus a correction for how its changes affect the other parts. A coordinator checks every proposal against the global objective and merges proposals that do not interfere with each other. We prove linear convergence up to an error floor that vanishes when the corrections are exact. Interfering proposals can also be merged once their combination has been checked; the rate then depends on how well the proposals actually combine, not on worst-case interactions. With GPT-OSS-120B agents on robot routing, a three-agent team finds collision-free plans in every run, better than those of a single agent that controls all robots. On a larger routing task, a six-agent team completes its search about twice as fast as the single agent (in median). Merging the best checked combination of proposals, rather than only proposals that do not interfere, cuts the team's search time by about 30%. With constants measured on the runs, our convergence bound holds in every round and follows the observed progress. We also test the theory on query planning in SQLite and on a synthetic task.

Abstract
Beam hopping technology enables LEO satellites to dynamically switch beam coverage positions, providing flexible resource allocation for non-uniformly distributed traffic demands. To address this problem, in this letter, we propose an interference-graph-aided multi-agent reinforcement learning approach that constructs an interference graph to capture beam coupling relationships and injects this structural prior into agent-side representation learning. Hybrid Belief Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Coordinated Spatial Exploration
Authors: Danish Rizvi, David Boyle
First: 2026-03-04T00:00:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T13:42:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Coordinating multiple autonomous agents to explore and serve spatially heterogeneous demand requires jointly learning unknown spatial patterns and planning trajectories that maximize task performance. Pure model-based approaches provide structured uncertainty estimates but lack adaptive policy learning, while deep reinforcement learning often suffers from poor sample efficiency when spatial priors are absent. This paper presents a hybrid belief-reinforcement learning (HBRL) framework to address this gap. In the first phase, agents construct spatial beliefs using a Log-Gaussian Cox Process (LGCP) and execute information-driven trajectories guided by a Pathwise Mutual Information (PathMI) planner with multi-step lookahead. In the second phase, trajectory control is transferred to a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent, warm-started through dual-channel knowledge transfer: i) belief state transfer supplies spatial uncertainty, and ii) replay buffer seeding provides demonstration trajectories generated during LGCP exploration. A variance-normalized overlap penalty enables coordinated coverage through shared belief state, permitting cooperative sensing in high-uncertainty regions while discouraging redundant coverage in well-explored areas. The framework is instantiated in two scenarios: multi-Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) wireless service provisioning, and mobility-on-demand sensing over measured New York City taxi demand. In the wireless scenario it achieves 10.8% higher cumulative reward and takes 38% fewer episodes to reach the reward level of the baselines, with ablation studies confirming that dual-channel transfer outperforms either channel alone; the taxi scenario yields consistent gains with all algorithmic components unchanged.

Abstract
Accurate electrical load forecasting supports power-system operation across countries, regions, cities, and buildings, but repeated development of target-specific models is costly. Time series foundation models (TSFMs) provide reusable pretrained backbones, although multi-domain pretraining may not fully capture load-specific characteristics. This paper compares pretrained inference, full fine-tuning, and low-rank adaptation (LoRA) in terms of forecasting accuracy and adaptation efficiency across four electrical load aggregation levels. MOIRAI 1.0 Large serves as the primary backbone, while Chronos-2 assesses whether the findings transfer across model families. The evaluation covers both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings and examines whether compact adaptation provides consistent gains over strong pretrained checkpoints. Pretrained foundation models outperform the implemented seasonal naive and XGBoost baselines across the evaluated aggregation levels. On MOIRAI Large, rank-16 LoRA reduces normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) by 7.5% on average while updating 3.146 million parameters, about 1% of the model, and retains most of the improvement from full fine-tuning. Chronos-2 achieves lower pretrained NMAE than MOIRAI but benefits less from adaptation. OOD results show that adaptation benefits vary by target, with the clearest gains for the local-area series and several buildings. Overall, LoRA provides a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off when adaptation yields a meaningful gain over a strong pretrained checkpoint.

Abstract
Object-centric reinforcement learning agents typically receive each object's position in a fixed global frame, even though their decisions usually depend on where objects are relative to one another. These absolute views often fail to capture the local context that is most relevant to the agent's behavior. As a result, an agent must relearn the same response at every location where a configuration can occur, and its performance collapses when the scene is merely shifted. In this work, we propose a shift toward Object-Relative Coordinates fixed to Anchor (ORCA), where entities are described in a relative frame of reference. To operationalize this idea, we introduce Object-Relative inductive-Bias Transformers (ORBiT), a transformer-based architecture for processing anchor-relative inputs in reinforcement learning. Our results suggest these relative representations improve performance for both MLP and transformer agents. Furthermore, we demonstrate that ORBiT outperforms the pixel-based PPO baseline, achieving an aggregate HNS IQM greater than 1.0 over 13 Atari games. ORBiT learns the game of Foosball from scratch, dominating all baselines. In our controlled Tag environment, we further show that ORCA extends to rotations, that ORBiT generalizes to unseen numbers of distractors, and that these gains require a decision-relevant anchor.

First: 2026-05-11T15:59:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T13:03:32+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurlPS 2026. Code is available at https://github.com/LAMDA-CL/NeurIPS2026-Drape

Abstract
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve strong performance through instruction tuning, yet real-world deployment often requires continual capability expansion across sequential tasks. In such scenarios, Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT) aims to acquire new capabilities while limiting catastrophic forgetting. Existing methods mainly follow a module-composition paradigm: they maintain task-level prompts or LoRA experts and dynamically route or aggregate a subset of them at inference. However, samples within the same task can still differ substantially in visual scenes, question intents, and reasoning demands. This motivates instance-level adaptation to individual query-image pairs rather than only selecting or combining task-level modules. To this end, we propose DRAPE (Dynamic Cross-Modal Prompt Generation), a prompt-learning framework that synthesizes continuous instance-specific soft prompts for MCIT. Instead of selecting prompts from a fixed pool, DRAPE derives prompt queries from the textual instruction and cross-attends to visual patch features, producing prompts conditioned on query-image pairs that are prepended to the frozen LLM. To mitigate forgetting during sequential updates, DRAPE applies null-space gradient projection to the shared projector and uses CLIP-based prototype routing for task-label-free generator selection at inference. Extensive experiments on MCIT benchmarks show that DRAPE achieves state-of-the-art performance among representative prompt-based and LoRA-based continual-learning baselines. Causal Discovery for Linear Models with Latent Confounding via Multi-domain Identifiable Representation Learning

Abstract
Causal discovery with latent confounding is particularly challenging, but a common setting in real-world data. In this work, we focus on linear causal models with latent confounding, represented by acyclic directed mixed graphs (ADMGs). Existing methods that fully identify ADMGs typically assume non-Gaussianity or graphical constraints, such as bow-freeness. In contrast, we propose Linear ADMG Learning (LinADMG), a framework for learning ADMGs for linear causal models through identifiable representation learning. Rather than relying on non-Gaussianity or bow-freeness, LinADMG leverages multi-domain settings to identify the noise variables. We show that the identifiability of the noise variables is sufficient for recovering all direct causal relations, and that, under an additional latent-confounding no-cancellation condition, LinADMG can identify the whole ADMG. We implement LinADMG with four identifiable representation learning approaches from the literature and validate the methods on simulated and semisynthetic datasets.

Abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models can learn a wide range of robotic skills, but combining separately trained skills remains challenging. The original training data may be unavailable for joint retraining, while directly merging model weights can cause interference. We propose NestedVLA, which consolidates independently fine-tuned VLA policies without access to their original training data. Instead of merging all updates into one checkpoint, a hypernetwork embedded in the VLA generates action-expert weight updates for a selected task. KuaFu: Compressing Long User Behavior into Understanding at Billion Scale
Authors: Jiahao Hui, Lin Zhu, Ben Tan, Yishen Hu, Jingdong Shu, Zetai Jiang, Xining Ran, Yeshou Cai, Gong Chen, Haijie Gu
First: 2026-09-25T09:39:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T12:09:04+00:00
Comments: 12 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Learning user preferences from long behavioral sequences is fundamental to large-scale advertising, recommender systems, and conversational agents. In industrial systems, preference modeling is typically task-wise, with different downstream tasks independently processing the same user history to capture different aspects of preference. This repeated processing creates substantial redundancy at billion-user scale, especially as preferences evolve and representations require frequent updates. We present KuaFu, a unified user preference modeling system that replaces repeated user-level sequence encoding with reusable item-level compressed representations. Each behavioral item is encoded once and shared across users and downstream tasks, while a user's history is built chronologically and extended with new item representations as they arrive, letting KuaFu capture evolving preferences without re-encoding the entire history. Abstract
Safe robot navigation in unknown, dynamic environments requires more than geometric collision checking: a robot still far from an obstacle may be unable to avoid it because of its speed, orientation, or the obstacle's motion. Hamilton--Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis captures this in a value function whose sign separates states that admit a safe continuation from those where collision is unavoidable, but computing it online for every newly observed environment is too expensive. This dissertation trains neural implicit representations offline on reachability solutions to give optimization-based controllers this information in real time. Since the value function never exceeds the constraint, it equals a directly computed constraint, such as a signed distance, minus a nonnegative dynamics correction. Learning only this correction keeps represented obstacles outside the predicted safe set despite approximation errors. The first two of five methods use the learned function as a terminal constraint in model predictive control, for static local environments and predicted obstacle motion. The next two use it as a barrier candidate in control barrier function safety filters, for static environments and, through pairwise barriers in relative coordinates, for a varying number of moving obstacles. Simulations and hardware experiments on ground and aerial robots show better collision avoidance than geometric and hand-designed baselines, especially for short horizons and moving obstacles. The fifth method replaces the grid-based offline solver, which limits the first four to low-dimensional models. For control-affine dynamics with polytopic inputs, the HJ Hamiltonian is maximized at a vertex, so a parallelizable tree search over vertex inputs can generate training labels. With a loss from the HJ variational inequality, these labels yield barrier candidates for systems with up to 13 states.
