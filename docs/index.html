Pushing Dexterity and Safety for In-Hand Object Pose Reaching That Works Remarkably Well
Authors: Ian Chuang, Hiya Shah, Vamsi Chilamkur, Iman Soltani, Claire Tomlin
First: 2026-10-27T17:58:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:58:20+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, 30 figures, 14 tables. Project page: https://ian-chuang.github.io/dexsafe/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands is the next frontier of robotics, yet learned hand skills remain limited to simple grasps or basic repeated motions, such as rotating an object. As robots attempt more demanding in-hand maneuvers, safety, such as not dropping the object, becomes critical, yet it is rarely measured. We study in-hand object pose reaching, a task that demands diverse and difficult grasps: moving an object to almost any position and orientation within reach of the fingers, at any wrist orientation, through long sequences of consecutive goals. We present a recipe for training such policies for a LEAP hand in simulation without human data, built on a range of insights, from diverse resets and goals via generated grasp datasets to an adaptive friction curriculum. With careful system identification, the policies transfer zero-shot to the real hand, using either motion capture or a single wrist-mounted depth camera, and prove remarkably capable and robust. On top of the task policy, we add a learned residual safety filter with adjustable conservativeness, letting us tune the balance between performance and safety and greatly reduce object drops. In 13.3 hours of real-robot evaluation over 4,800 unseen goals across 4 objects and diverse wrist orientations, the state-based policy reaches 95.2% of goals within 2 cm and 0.4 rad, and the filter cuts its drops by 75% while still reaching 91.5% of goals. The policies can also spin a lightbulb held in an arbitrary pose, reach out-of-distribution goals, withstand fast arm motion and perturbations, and, needing only a wrist camera, serve as portable skills such as screwing in a lightbulb. We release the full recipe as a platform for studying performance, reliability, and safety in high-dimensional dexterous systems. Videos and more are available at https://ian-chuang.github.io/dexsafe/.

EnvSched: Environment-Aware Scheduling for Rollout-Efficient Agent Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Sihan Yang, Weichen Bai, Ruichuan An, Yiming Cheng, Zhiyuan Feng, Tianyi Jiang, Renye Yan, Ning Ding, Yifan Zhang, Renrui Zhang, Yehui Tang, Wentao Zhang
First: 2026-10-27T17:38:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:38:52+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
As executable environments for language agents scale, generating and verifying their rollouts becomes increasingly expensive. Under finite rollout budgets, this raises an important question: can data management at the environment level make agentic reinforcement learning (RL) more efficient by organizing, evaluating, and scheduling task instances together with the environments that determine their tools, states, transitions, and verification? To address this question, we introduce EnvSched (Environment-aware Scheduling), an online scheduler that learns from rollout history. EnvSched assigns scheduling value through two complementary modeling choices: exploitation prioritizes tasks likely to provide informative reward differences, while coverage spreads each batch across underobserved environments and interaction patterns. EnvSched organizes environment--task pairs as nodes in a dual-relation graph, with within-environment edges capturing task semantics and shared tool, state, and verifier structure, and cross-environment edges capturing abstract execution and reward topology. By propagating historical rollout statistics along both edge types, it predicts within-task reward variance. These predictions guide a scheduler that allocates each batch to tasks with high predicted reward variance and underobserved environments and tasks. Environment quotas and a similarity penalty further limit concentration and redundancy, yielding informative and diverse batches without additional pilot rollouts. In RL on Qwen3-8B, EnvSched achieves an average score of 48.81 across BFCL-v3, tau-Bench, and ACEBench-Agent after 40 training steps, exceeding the highest score attained by random task selection after 120 steps. It also improves downstream performance across model sizes at the same training budget.

Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR
Authors: Peize Ding
First: 2026-10-27T17:01:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:01:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Label-free reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) rewards a language model for agreeing with its own majority vote. It improves reasoning at first but eventually collapses: the self-assessed reward goes to one while accuracy falls, often until the model gives the same answer to every problem. The collapse appears only when training uses many problems. We explain this dataset-size dependence as a collective instability. In a solvable many-problem model in which problems share parameters, a population whose problems prefer different answers amplifies any shared perturbation for as long as the population-averaged slope $\langle F'\rangle$ of the single-problem self-reinforcement drift $F$ is positive. This holds while problems are still uncertain about their answers. Per-problem sharpening, slowed by $1/N$ for $N$ training problems, races against the shared mode, which grows at a rate $\proptoλ^2$ independent of $N$, where $λ$ is the strength of the shared coupling. This yields a critical number of problems $N_c\simeq\ln(1/δ)/(λ^2G)$, where $δ$ seeds the shared mode and $G$ decreases with the initial confidence. Simulations of the model confirm the predictions: a sharp transition, $N_c\proptoλ^{-2}$ up to a logarithmic correction, a single critical amplification $λ^2NG$ across couplings, confidence profiles and training variants, a logarithmic dependence on batch size, and no collapse for confident initial models. A kernel version of the theory identifies a protective coupling (generalization of sharpening) and a marginal-entropy bonus that stabilizes the uniform collective mode. In label-free RL of a small Transformer, removing the shared direction prevents collapse entirely, restoring it causes collapse, and collapse grows with training-set size and base-model entropy.

Efficient Token-Level Policy Optimization for Masked Diffusion Models via Velocity Alignment
Authors: Chenxing Wei, Jiazhen Kang, Hong Wang, Jianqing Zhang, Hao Jiang, Xiaolong Xu, Ningyuan Sun, Ying He, F. Richard Yu, Yao Shu, Bo Jiang
First: 2026-03-02T07:42:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:51:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) successfully aligns autoregressive models in rigorous domains like mathematical reasoning and code generation. However, applying RLVR to Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) is hindered by intractable sequence-level likelihoods, forcing existing methods to rely on unstable, high-variance approximations. We propose Sequence Likelihood-Free Policy Optimization (LFPO), an efficient framework that circumvents this bottleneck by formulating policy alignment as discrete velocity alignment. Inspired by continuous flow matching, LFPO eschews trajectory-level estimation and directly optimizes denoising logits via contrastive token-level updates, providing a stable, low-variance surrogate. Furthermore, predicting terminal solutions from intermediate states implicitly straightens the generation trajectory, enabling high-quality sampling with significantly fewer denoising iterations. Extensive experiments on reasoning and coding benchmarks demonstrate that LFPO matches or exceeds the accuracy of likelihood-based baselines while substantially accelerating inference.

Fast Fault-Tolerant Decoders for Hypergraph Product and Lifted-Product Codes
Authors: Asit Kumar Pradhan, Nithin Raveendran, David Declercq, Bane Vasić
First: 2026-08-31T16:18:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:30:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We design low-complexity, fault-tolerant decoders for quantum low-density parity-check (QLDPC) codes with the goal of reducing decoding latency. We target two major bottlenecks of decoding under the \emph{circuit-level} noise model: (i) post-processing via order-statistics decoding (OSD), and (ii) the large number of auxiliary variable nodes commonly introduced to represent CNOT-induced correlations during syndrome extraction. Our key observation is that propagating CNOT faults (\emph{hook errors}) create \emph{stabilizer-induced} trapping sets (TSs) that are intrinsic to hypergraph-product (HGP) and lifted-product (LP) constructions. Therefore, instead of modeling each such fault with an explicit correlation node and relying on OSD to clean up the resulting failures, we design message-passing decoders that resolve the corresponding \emph{stabilizer-induced} TSs directly. We obtain these decoders by deriving QLDPC decoders from decoders for the parent classical LDPC codes and using them collectively to correct broad families of \emph{stabilizer-induced} TSs. For CNOT faults that manifest primarily as syndrome errors, we show that their effect is equivalent to a data error together with syndrome-bit measurement errors. Consequently, given repeated measurements and a decoding graph that already includes nodes representing syndrome-bit errors, no distinct variable node is needed for each CNOT fault. Using a \emph{phenomenological} Tanner graph with nodes representing only data errors and syndrome-bit errors, simulations on LP codes show a significant improvement in logical error rate relative to BP+OSD. Moreover, the improved error correction relies on low-complexity finite-state iterative decoders, targeted for fast and low-latency FPGA implementation.

Coordinated and Uncoordinated Channel Tracking for High-Rate IoT in Distributed Massive MIMO With Low-Resolution ADCs
Authors: Hediyeh Soltanizadeh, Ardavan Rahimian
First: 2026-10-27T16:30:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:30:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper investigates dynamic channel tracking for high-rate Internet of things (IoT) in distributed massive multiple-input multiple-output (mMIMO) with low-resolution analog-to-digital converters (ADCs), where pilot and data activities are decoupled, pilots are reused, and mobile channels evolve across access slots. We hereby develop a quantization-aware joint coordinated Kalman filter (Q-JCKF) for known pilot activity and a quantization-aware distributed probabilistic data association filter (Q-DPDAF) for uncoordinated access. For uplink analysis, we derive a closed-form statistical use-and-then-forget (UatF) expression for Q-JCKF and mixture-aware, pilot-conditioned, and long-term statistical UatF formulations for Q-DPDAF, together with the large-N pilot-reuse ceiling and a cross-access point (AP) association term with an O(epsilon) posterior-concentration bound. The proposed framework recovers JC-KF/PDAF in the ideal-ADC co-located limit, and quantifies the conditional loss of Kalman measurement information caused by finite-resolution quantization. We further characterize computational, memory, and fronthaul scaling: Kronecker-structured Q-JCKF reduces covariance-processing complexity, while groupwise Q-DPDAF limits hypothesis enumeration from 2^K to 2^{K_r} and enables parallel AP-local processing. Simulations corroborate the analytical and complexity outcomes, quantify ADC-resolution and pilot-access effects, and demonstrate improved lower-tail spectral efficiency, robust mobility tracking, near-ideal 4-bit performance, reliable Q-DPDAF activity inference, and substantial runtime reductions over legacy implementations and processing frameworks.

Authors: Stabak Das, Priyesh Ranjan, Xiangfang Li, Lijun Qian

Abstract
High-level robot actions such as goto(region) can hide intermediate traversal, allowing a monitor of the listed actions to accept a plan whose graph-level execution violates a safety rule. We present Graph-Aware Refinement and Verification (GARV), which composes graph-grounded action contracts with a safety monitor to produce a model-relative certificate or counterexample and, when route choice is enforceable, a minimum-cost safe refinement. In a 28-case controlled audit, refinement exposed all 12 targeted hidden hazards and produced the expected verdicts on 16 controls; a separate 14-case SPINE-RoboGuard instantiation yielded two false-safe cases and five action-interface errors. In standalone finite-graph simulation, all 16 designed cases met their expected outcomes, and product verification agreed with exhaustive enumeration on classification, minimum safe hop count, and witness safety in 160 comparisons across 40 graphs. Across 90 graph-contract evaluations on 30 graphs with a hazard deliberately placed on the selected shortest route, GARV found a safe refinement in 72 evaluations and no safe route within the permitted hop bound in 18. For the static forbidden-region rule, synthesis matched a simple hazard-deletion baseline in all 90 evaluations; GARV's guarantees remain conditional on the semantic graph, action contract, safety specification, and enforceability of any selected route.

Abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. Abstract
Integrated sensing and communication allows wireless networks to recognize human activity from the channel estimates that the receiver computes anyway. The inference, however, runs on the general-purpose processors of the base station, access point or handset, which are the first components a device switches off to save energy. Always-on sensing therefore requires a classifier that is small enough to remain powered while the rest of the system sleeps. We investigate whether Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) can provide such a classifier. Interpretable Meta-Policies for Neural Decision Making
Authors: Raban Emunds, Jannis Blüml, Quentin Delfosse, Kristian Kersting

Abstract
Deep Reinforcement Learning remains difficult to interpret and align due to the black-box nature of neural networks. While (neuro-)symbolic approaches improve transparency by incorporating symbolic reasoning, applying them directly to low-level actions results in overly complex, unreadable policies. We introduce NEXUS, a hierarchical RL framework that resolves this tension by combining neural skills with symbolic meta-policies. By shifting transparent reasoning to high-level skills rather than atomic actions, NEXUS drastically reduces symbolic complexity without sacrificing environment performance. Object-centric observations enable high-level reasoning and the design of reward functions and meta-policies by interaction with LLMs. We demonstrate that NEXUS agents learn disentangled skills, are interpretable regarding high-level goals, are better aligned to goals described in the game manual, less prone to reward hacking and more robust to game simplifications than deep RL. Finally, we isolate and evaluate the impact of different meta-policy architectures (neural, symbolic, neuro-symbolic) on environmental return and aligned task performance.

DRIFT: A Residual Flow Adapter for Decoding Continuous Outputs in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Zhuoming Liu, Jinhong Lin, Kwan Man Cheng, Lin Zhang, Shayok Bagchi, Yin Li
First: 2026-06-04T06:37:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:50:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many modern vision-language models (VLMs) build on autoregressive decoding of discrete tokens. While text-based output interfaces enable scalable pretraining and strong zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks, they are poorly suited for problems that require precise continuous outputs, such as localizing temporal boundaries of events or generating robotic control actions. To address this challenge, we propose DRIFT, a general framework for adapting pretrained VLMs to continuous decoding tasks. DRIFT combines a base predictor, which provides a coarse estimate of the target output, with a generative refinement module based on flow matching that iteratively improves the prediction. This residual formulation transforms the generative modeling problem from learning a global output distribution to modeling a localized residual distribution around a strong prior, substantially simplifying optimization. We evaluate DRIFT on both perception and planning tasks, including visual grounding and robotic control. Across multiple tasks and architectures spanning MLLMs, VLAs, and WAMs, DRIFT consistently outperforms a strong set of regression- and generative-based solutions.

Abstract
Objective: This study aims to determine whether a hip-exoskeleton policy trained in simulation, by co-adapting with a musculoskeletal human model under a metabolic-energy reward, reduces the metabolic cost of walking in new users without per-user tuning, and how mechanical-power shaping affects the learned assistance. Methods: We developed MARL-Exo, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework in which a full-body human model (304 muscle actuators; randomized anatomy, gait, and speed) and a bilateral hip-exoskeleton policy are trained concurrently in two stages. The exoskeleton observes only delayed hip kinematics and its past commands; its reward includes muscle metabolic power from a physiologically based energetics model, alone (Metabolic) or with mechanical-power shaping (Metabolic + Power). The Metabolic policy was deployed and tested without retraining in eight healthy adults walking at 1.25 m/s, using indirect calorimetry. Results: In simulation, the Metabolic policy reduced modeled muscle metabolic power by 13.8% relative to no-device walking, versus 8.8% for Metabolic + Power. On hardware, MARL-Exo reduced net metabolic rate by 18.3 $\pm$ 6.6% relative to Passive in all eight participants. Relative to No-Exo, the mean reduction was 7.4 $\pm$ 10.0%; six participants more than offset the 13.1% device-wearing penalty, walking up to 24.2% below their No-Exo cost. Conclusion: A metabolically informed policy co-adapted in simulation transferred to users without per-user tuning and consistently reduced the metabolic cost of walking with the device, while explicit power shaping reduced the simulated benefit. Significance: Unlike per-user human-in-the-loop optimization, this approach yields a single population-level controller and allows assistance objectives to be screened in simulation before human testing.

Abstract
Modern simulators can generate synthetic camera and LiDAR data with accurate annotations for downstream tasks. In contrast, automotive radar simulation remains less mature because it requires not only a 3D engine to model dynamic objects in the scenes but also electromagnetic material properties, antenna configurations, wave propagation mechanisms, and coherent signal synthesis. Existing frameworks are either proprietary or overly simplified, limiting the scalability of radar raw measurements. Assessing BIOMASS Radiometric Stability using Permanent Scatterers
Authors: Marco Manzoni, Francesco Salvaterra, Claudio Maria Prati, Stefano Tebaldini

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Monitoring the relative radiometric stability of the European Space Agency's BIOMASS P-band synthetic aperture radar (SAR) requires separating instrumental and processing effects from temporal changes in the scene backscatter. This study investigates the use of permanent scatterers (PSs) as opportunistic references for estimating residual radiometric gains without dedicated calibration targets. The PS-based calibration (PSCal) framework combines amplitude-based scatterer selection with iterative maximum-likelihood gain estimation. Simulations assess detection and estimation performance under the limited number of acquisitions available in BIOMASS stacks, highlighting the importance of stringent selection thresholds and the dependence of estimation accuracy on scatterer abundance and signal-to-noise ratio. The approach is applied to datasets over Nairobi, Kenya, and Cordoba, Argentina. Over Nairobi, PSCal identifies an approximately 1 dB radiometric anomaly subsequently traced to a defect in radio-frequency interference mitigation, while the remaining acquisitions exhibit estimated gain variations generally within 0.1 dB. Over Cordoba, twelve acquisitions spanning two mission phases separated by approximately eight months yield residual gain variations on the order of 0.1 dB despite pronounced changes in the scene backscatter and acquisition geometry. Results were observed to be consistent with measurements from the BIOMASS Calibration Transponder (BCT) installed in New Norcia (Australia). These results demonstrate the potential of carefully selected P-band PSs to complement dedicated calibration infrastructure for relative radiometric monitoring and the detection of processing anomalies.

Abstract
Robot policy evaluation often relies on a binary success signal for task completion. In real-world deployment, however, desired execution behaviors are also defined by quality attributes such as execution speed and motion smoothness, which need to be dynamically controlled according to task requirements. To address this, we introduce Quality-Controllable Policy (QCP), a robot policy that enables inference-time control over both individual quality attributes and their combinations. Hard Constraints, Smooth Gradients: Learning Feasible Inventory Policies via Differentiable Projection
Authors: Patrick Helm, Jan-Niklas Doerr, Joren Gijsbrechts, Stefan Minner
First: 2026-08-03T14:57:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:59:25+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many operational problems are constrained sequential decision processes with combinatorial action spaces and interdependent constraints. Mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs) handle such constraints flexibly but scale poorly in stochastic environments. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) promises scalable decision rules, but existing methods either penalize constraints rather than enforce them, or rely on feasibility mechanisms that break down once constraints interact. We bridge this gap by embedding a differentiable optimization module inside the policy: a neural network proposes continuous action targets, a quadratic program projects them onto the relaxed feasible set, and a dual-informed integer mapping restores integrality while preserving feasibility. Given a differentiable simulator, the policy trains end-to-end from sampled trajectories using pathwise gradients, while handling hard constraints with similar flexibility to MILPs. We show that our approach has bounded error relative to an exact integer projection and ensures the entire feasible action space is reachable. We apply the method to multi-echelon production-inventory planning under shared resource and material constraints. Our policy attains an average optimality gap of 1% on 3-item instances and consistently outperforms a rolling-horizon multi-stage stochastic program by at least 5.6% in 10-item networks. Compared to state-of-the-art echelon base-stock policies, savings reach 15.3% on 40-item instances. On an industry-scale case study with nonstationary demand from ASML, we reduce average cost by up to 1.8% relative to the best-known benchmark policy. The savings are largest where planning is hardest: in tightly capacitated systems with high demand variability. More broadly, we show that DRL can improve upon state-of-the-art baselines in sequential decision problems with hard constraints, which are widespread in practice.

Abstract
Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) is well suited to large-deformation fluid problems, and the growing use of automatic differentiation in engineering applications has motivated interest in differentiable SPH solvers. In this study, a composable differentiable SPH framework, CraftSPH, has been developed. In CraftSPH, major computational operations are implemented as independent modules that users can combine to construct solvers for different purposes. This design supports high-accuracy discretization and both explicit and implicit computation within a common framework, while allowing individual components to be maintained and extended. Selected numerical operations also support automatic differentiation (AD) for physical-parameter estimation and potential coupling with deep learning models. These capabilities were demonstrated through forward and inverse analyses. Forward analyses using an implicit SPH scheme with high-accuracy discretization showed good agreement with analytical and reference solutions for Poiseuille flow and a rising bubble, while capturing the key experimental trends in two- and three-dimensional dam-break problems. In inverse analyses, explicit and implicit SPH schemes were used to estimate kinematic viscosity in Taylor-Green vortex flow and Reynolds number in lid-driven cavity flow. The results also showed that the choice of operations included in the computational graph affects the computed gradients. Together, these findings demonstrate that CraftSPH enables users to assemble problem-specific solvers for forward and inverse analyses within a common framework.

Abstract
Explanations of LLM outputs are considered an important way to make model behavior legible, helping human users make good decisions on downstream tasks. However, default training approaches for improving explanations target model task performance or human preference rather than human decision quality. To bridge this gap, we introduce \textbf{HD-RL}, a reinforcement learning framework for optimizing LLM explanations for human decision making. Abstract
Analog device sizing must satisfy all specifications across process, voltage, and temperature (PVT) corners to ensure tape-out success, but simulating every candidate at all corners dominates the sizing time and slows design iteration. Conventional surrogate-based optimizers search the sizing space directly, while recent large language model (LLM) agents add circuit knowledge to designs but still delegate the sizes to global optimizers. Both learn the circuit response from repeated multi-corner SPICE simulations and optimize on top of it, which becomes expensive under a limited simulation budget. Providing an agent with reusable verified designs and sizing sub-blocks analytically could save most of this budget. We propose AnalogScout, a simulation-efficient PVT-aware sizing framework driven by an LLM agent. Combining elite-design reuse, analytic gm/Id local sizing and global refinement under full-corner verification, AnalogScout reduces power by 43.3% relative to the evolution strategy CMA-ES at an equal budget of 16k simulations on three SKY130 OTAs of increasing complexity, and reaches the final CMA-ES power after 7.0% of that budget. It also attains lower power than Bayesian optimization on all three OTAs and than two re-implemented LLM-driven flows on the two OTAs where they were tested.

Abstract

Abstract
Multi-trigger backdoor attacks, in which heterogeneous trigger mechanisms (e.g., patch, blend, warping, and the latent-domain Spy-Watermark) coexist within a single poisoned model, cannot be addressed by existing shortcut-based defenses that assume a single, static attack mechanism. Current multi-trigger remedies (BU+FT, BU+ANP, and BU+ABL) rely on backdoor unlearning, which incurs high training costs and loses effectiveness at low poisoning ratios. This paper proposes Combinatorial Lock, a training-free, reference-calibrated defense that reduces multi-trigger identification to a combinatorial problem over binary activation signatures. This formulation is the key novelty: it yields a multi-trigger defense that is training-free, trigger-oblivious, and directly implementable in hardware, since its decision rules can be evaluated with integer and logic operations. The framework combines anomaly-gated spectral scrubbing, which removes the malicious subspace only from inputs identified as anomalous, with a hash-compressed combinatorial signature verified by an ensemble of detection experts, including a compound-attack-aware expert for multi-trigger inputs. A joint threshold-shrinkage search identifies an operating point ($τ$ at the 97.1st percentile, $γ= 0.78$) that reduces the attack success rate (ASR) to as low as 0.1% while preserving 97.8% clean accuracy. Evaluation on six models (ResNet18, VGG16, MobileNet, AlexNet, ViT-Small, ViT-Base) shows strong generalization: near-100% ASR drops to single digits across poisoning rates of 1-10% and 1-9 simultaneous triggers, with only gradual degradation in clean accuracy. ASIC and FPGA implementations confirm practicality, adding a consistent 19-20% latency overhead across all evaluated architectures and establishing Combinatorial Lock as a hardware-native approach to multi-trigger backdoor mitigation.

Abstract
Proprietary language models and their reasoning traces are vulnerable to API-based distillation, where an adversary trains a student model on the teacher model's outputs. To obtain evidence of distillation, model providers can watermark the teacher's outputs, aiming to leave a radioactive signal in the student's outputs. Existing black-box detectors search for such radioactivity using token-level statistics of the suspect model's outputs. However, token-level signals are not always detectable, for instance when the distillation traces are sampled at a low temperature. In this work, we discover the watermark basin, a persistent signal that enables more reliable watermark detection: during fine-tuning, watermarked teacher traces embed a distinct, key-specific geometric signal in parameter space that lies orthogonal to the ordinary task-specific fine-tuning subspace. Building on this, we introduce Watermark Basin Alignment (WM-Basin), a black-box watermark detector that queries the suspect model through its API, without access to its weights or logits. WM-Basin maps generated text to gradient representations using a fixed clean probe model, projects out the ordinary fine-tuning task directions, and tests whether the residual gradient aligns with the watermark basin induced by the watermark key. We evaluate WM-Basin extensively across diverse teacher models and student architectures (from 135M to 8.2B parameters) distilled on mathematical reasoning. At a strict 0.1% false-positive rate, WM-Basin detects 79% of distilled models at n-gram=1 and 45% at n-gram=3, substantially improving over the state-of-the-art token-level baseline (29% and 1.3%, respectively). Because the watermark signal resides in a persistent basin, it survives post-distillation modifications including quantization, pruning, and reinforcement-learning post-training.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a paradigm for fine-tuning large-scale generative models, such as diffusion and flow models, to align with complex human preferences and user-specified tasks. A fundamental limitation remains \textit{the curse of diversity collapse}, where the objective formulation and optimization landscape inherently collapse the policy to a Dirac delta distribution. To address this challenge, we propose \textbf{DRIFT} (\textbf{D}ive\textbf{R}sity-\textbf{I}ncentivized Reinforcement \textbf{F}ine-\textbf{T}uning for Versatile Image Generation), an innovative framework that systematically incentivizes output diversity throughout the on-policy fine-tuning process, reconciling strong task alignment with high generation diversity to enhance versatility essential for applications that demand diverse candidate generations. We approach the problem across three representative perspectives: i) \textbf{sampling} a reward-concentrated subset that filters out reward outliers to prevent premature collapse; ii) \textbf{prompting} with stochastic variations to expand the conditioning space, and iii) \textbf{optimization} of the intra-group diversity with a potential-based reward shaping mechanism. Experimental results show that DRIFT exhibits clear Pareto dominance in task alignment and generation diversity, achieving 3.88\%$\sim$81.42\% higher diversity at matched alignment and 14.38\%$\sim$56.45\% higher alignment at matched diversity. Abstract
Reinforcement learning for long-horizon agents relies on feedback from sequences of interactions, making credit assignment to individual actions difficult. Existing methods such as GiGPO compare actions at shared states to compute relative advantages, but estimate each action's value from a single sampled continuation. The resulting local credit remains sensitive to decisions made later in the trajectory. To address this problem, we propose ConCal (Continuation Calibration), a recursive continuation-value estimator that incorporates continuation evidence already present in a rollout batch. ConCal matches visits from the same task by their incoming state-action-state transitions, combines shared continuation evidence with each visit's own transition estimate, and propagates calibrated values backward to refine earlier action-value estimates. This reduces dependence on individual sampled continuations and mitigates local credit distortions without additional rollouts. Held-out return diagnostics show lower error in within-group value differences and higher agreement with reference action orderings than estimates based on individual continuations. With Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, ConCal improves success rates over GiGPO by 3.39 and 5.86 percentage points on ALFWorld and WebShop, respectively. Our code is available at https://github.com/Yingjian-Chen/ConCal.

Abstract
Reliable probability estimates are critical in many machine learning applications, yet modern classifiers are often poorly calibrated. Post-hoc calibration provides a simple and widely used solution, but the large number of proposed methods, combined with small-scale and inconsistent evaluations, makes it difficult to determine which approaches are truly effective in practice. We introduce a large-scale, standardized benchmark for post-hoc calibration, covering nearly 2000 experiments across tabular and computer vision tasks, including binary, multiclass, and large-scale classification settings. Our benchmark aggregates predictions from a diverse set of classical models, modern deep learning architectures, and foundation models, and provides unified, reproducible implementations of dozens of calibration methods within a common evaluation framework. We argue that Post-Hoc Improvement (PHI) in proper scoring rules offers a principled alternative to traditional calibration error estimators for comparing post-hoc methods, capturing both calibration quality and potential degradation to the model's predictive performance. Using this framework, we conduct the most comprehensive empirical study of post-hoc calibration to date. Our results reveal consistent patterns across domains: smooth calibration functions outperform binning-based approaches, dedicated multiclass methods are essential in high-dimensional settings, and generic machine learning models are not competitive without calibration-specific design. To facilitate future research, we release all data, code, and evaluation tools, providing a plug-and-play benchmark for developing and comparing calibration methods.

Abstract
Illegal and unsustainable wildlife trade remains a major conservation challenge and is increasingly shifting to online marketplaces where monitoring is difficult. To address this gap, we used real-world images from online platforms, enforcement seizures, and market inspections to develop a deep-learning object detection framework to identify products derived from three highly traded species: elephant, pangolin, and tiger. Our dataset of 39,941 images included both wildlife products and visually similar non-wildlife items, reflecting real enforcement complexity. We used cross-entropy and asymmetric focal loss functions to train Faster Region-based convolutional neural network (Faster R-CNN) models and compared species-specific and multispecies training strategies. The best-performing model, trained with cross-entropy on all species combined, achieved an overall accuracy of 84.2%, with high performance for pangolin (93.6%) and tiger (90.3%) and moderate performance for elephant (71.1%). Although asymmetric focal loss increased recall, it substantially reduced precision. We deployed the optimized model in a prototype smartphone application, potentially usable in real-world settings for monitoring online images. With an independent test set, the application achieved 91.3% accuracy and an F-score of 0.86. These results demonstrate the potential of object detection models to support scalable, real-time monitoring of wildlife trade and enable the rapid triage of suspicious posts, regulatory compliance, and conservation decision-making.

Abstract
