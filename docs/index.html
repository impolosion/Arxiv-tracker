Coordination Matters: Process-Aware Evaluation of Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Maria Ana Cardei, Matthew Landers, Afsaneh Doryab
First: 2026-05-07T16:50:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:58:51+00:00
Comments: Under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) benchmarks commonly emphasize aggregate outcomes such as return, success rate, or completion time. While essential, these metrics can obscure how agents coordinate and fail to distinguish policies that achieve similar outcomes through different interaction processes. We propose a process-aware evaluation framework that complements aggregate performance with diagnostics of interference, coordinated task completion, participation, and coordination efficiency. We evaluate the framework in STAT, a controlled spatial task-allocation testbed, and examine its broader applicability in two established cooperative MARL benchmarks. Across diverse value-based and actor-critic methods, we find policies with nearly identical return can exhibit substantial differences in coordination behavior. Controlled scaling in STAT reveals distinct process signatures for spatial, task, and agent scaling, while a reward-shaping ablation shows that explicitly penalizing assignment conflicts can alter coordination behavior with little change in task return. Results in the established benchmarks further show that the framework remains informative under different coordination mechanisms. These findings support process-aware evaluation as a complement to outcome-based benchmarking. The framework provides a common structure for selecting and interpreting environment-specific diagnostics that characterize not only what cooperative policies achieve, but how they coordinate to achieve it.

Abstract
Diffusion has emerged as a powerful framework for generative modeling, achieving remarkable success in applications such as image and audio synthesis. Enlightened by this progress, we propose a novel diffusion-based approach for symbolic regression. We construct a random mask-based diffusion and denoising process to generate diverse and high-quality equations. We integrate this generative processes with a token-wise Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) method to conduct efficient reinforcement learning on the given measurement dataset. In addition, we introduce a long short-term risk-seeking policy to expand the pool of top-performing candidates, further enhancing performance. Extensive experiments and ablation studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of our approach.

Abstract
Designing RNA sequences that satisfy target tertiary-structure objectives is an important problem in RNA engineering, with broad applications in synthetic biology and therapeutics. Existing 3D RNA design methods have made substantial progress, but they largely treat the target fold as a geometry-only objective. This is incomplete for RNA, whose functional folds are specified not only by tertiary geometry but also by secondary base-pairing topology. We therefore formulate RNA inverse design as a topology-and-geometry co-design problem and propose TANGO, a multi-objective multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for this task. TANGO adopts a staged curriculum: it first learns a topology-aware policy from secondary-structure targets, then transfers this policy to tertiary-structure targets to train a general co-design model, and finally applies frontier-guided fine-tuning to refine target-specific trade-offs among secondary-structure fidelity, tertiary-structure fidelity, and sequence diversity. Experiments show that TANGO generates diverse and novel designs, improving tertiary- and secondary-structure performance by 18.3% and 52.7%, respectively, over the strongest baseline.

Learning without relearning physical symmetry: an SO(3)-equivariant convolutional network (CNN) on the sphere
Authors: Joshua Williamson, Niall Jeffrey, Lorne Whiteway
First: 2026-10-16T17:45:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:45:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many of the most important datasets in the physical sciences live natively on the sphere, and these data often carry no preferred position or orientation. Using methods that do not reflect this symmetry leads to far higher simulation requirements, which can be catastrophic for scientific analyses (forcing reliance on cheap, imperfect models that bias results). We present a novel spherical implementation of a gauge equivariant mesh architecture that is specifically designed for the Hierarchical Equal Area isoLatitude Pixelization (\healpix{}) of the sphere. The $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-equivariant convolution is built from steerable filters, parallel transport and spin-weighted features. The architecture supplies the exact symmetry, so the network does not spend training data learning it. The convolution operations are constructed to give provably exact equivariance, given a band-limit. Our full CNNs can achieve SO(3)-invariance error at per-cent-level and lower, which is controlled through well-understood hyperparameters. On benchmarks, our technique gives the most accurate dense gradient regression and competitive cosmic microwave background foreground removal and denoising. The improved simulation efficiency matters most in simulation-based inference (SBI), where high-fidelity simulations are expensive and neural compressors require large training sets, so a fixed budget forces analyses with less accurate simulators. In cosmology lensing SBI, our technique yields the tightest posteriors at matched simulation count. This approach is a novel method that will enable new convolutional neural network analyses, including generative and inference tasks, for processes and data on the sphere.

Abstract
Designing RNA sequences that reliably fold into specific secondary structures is essential for understanding their biological functions but remains a challenging computational problem. We propose CocoRNA, a cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for RNA inverse design. CocoRNA simplifies the design task by decomposing it into smaller sub-problems, each solved collaboratively by multiple agents. This approach reduces the complexity of the problem and improves the exploration of design policies. During training, a centralized critic uses global structural information to guide the agents, enabling them to jointly optimize their design strategies. Further experiments on other biological sequence design tasks highlight the effectiveness and broad potential of CocoRNA for complex design tasks.

Abstract
Predicting the compressive strength of fiber-reinforced high-performance concrete subjected to temperature exposure is challenging because to the intricate interdependencies of mixture composition, fiber properties, curing conditions, and temperature. A data-driven framework to predict compressive strength utilizing 1,204 experimental records containing a diverse array of material designs and exposure situations. This study evaluates eight machine-learning models and demonstrate that ensemble approaches routinely surpass individual learners. Both gradient boosting and extreme gradient boosting exhibit superior performance on unseen data, each attaining a R^2 of 0.903. Gradient boosting produces the minimal root mean square error (6.67 MPa), whereas extreme gradient boosting achieves the lowest mean absolute error (3.97 MPa) and mean absolute percentage error (10.60%). The models are further interpreted utilizing Shapley additive explanations and partial dependence analysis, which reveal temperature as the primary factor influencing compressive strength, succeeded by fine aggregate, superplasticizer, cement content, water-to-binder ratio, and water content. Strength diminishes significantly with rising temperature, albeit the extent of deterioration is contingent upon the specific combination design. The best performing models are subsequently implemented in a Python-based graphical user interface for expedited prediction. Our findings offer a precise and comprehensible methodology for evaluating temperature-dependent compressive strength and facilitate the practical, data-driven design of fiber-reinforced high-performance concrete.

Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models provide a natural language interface to robot control, but the mapping from language to behavior is often brittle and unintuitive: semantically similar instructions can induce drastically different behaviors, while some capabilities may not be elicitable through prompting alone. As a result, both human instructions and zero-shot language models can fail to reliably steer VLAs toward successful task execution. In this work, we propose a framework that interactively searches for language sequences that improve closed-loop VLA task performance, distills these sequences into a test-time language feedback policy (LFP), and learns an improvement head that predicts when language steering will improve performance. We conformalize this improvement head to prevent harmful steering interventions, where the LFP decreases task performance relative to the original instruction on out-of-distribution scenarios. Crucially, our approach operates on arbitrary frozen pre-trained VLAs, requiring neither access to the original training distribution nor fine-tuning of the underlying model. On seen environments, our conformalized LFP improves base VLA performance by 24.7% in simulation and 65.0% in hardware. On visual and semantic perturbations with calibration samples, our conformalized LFP has strong harmlessness guarantees, and produces recovery behaviors not observed with open-loop prompting.

Abstract
In unsupervised adaptation for vision-language models such as CLIP, pseudo-labels derived from zero-shot predictions often exhibit significant noise, particularly under domain shifts or in visually complex scenarios. Conventional pseudo-label filtering approaches, which rely on fixed confidence thresholds, tend to be unreliable in fully unsupervised settings. In this work, we propose a novel adaptive pseudo-labeling framework that enhances CLIP's adaptation performance by integrating prototype consistency and neighborhood-based consistency. The proposed method comprises two key components: PICS, which assesses pseudo-label accuracy based on in-class feature compactness and cross-class feature separation; and NALR, which exploits semantic similarities among neighboring samples to refine pseudo-labels dynamically. Additionally, we introduce an adaptive weighting mechanism that adjusts the influence of pseudo-labeled samples during training according to their estimated correctness. Extensive experiments on 11 benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised adaptation scenarios, delivering more accurate pseudo-labels while maintaining computational efficiency.

Abstract
Weakly supervised anomaly detection on tabular data is an essential task in many label-scarce applications, since tabular data is widely encountered and it is commonly observed in practice that only a limited number of labeled anomalies are available alongside abundant unlabeled samples. Most existing methods try to learn features that capture the inherent distribution patterns in the target domain. However, such features often struggle to provide a complete description of possible abnormal characteristics and are highly susceptible to overfitting the extremely scarce labels. To address this challenge, we propose a weakly supervised anomaly detection method based on universal semantic enhancement for tabular data, which fuses the universal knowledge from a pre-trained tabular foundation model with the domain-specific knowledge learned on the target dataset. The two types of knowledge are naturally complementary: universal semantics describe the common patterns of tabular data across domains, while the domain-specific features capture the inherent distributions of normal and anomalous behaviors in the target dataset. A prototype-conditioned modulation mechanism is then designed to effectively integrate the two types of features, allowing them to reinforce each other for improved anomaly detection. Extensive experiments on public datasets show that the proposed method outperforms competitive methods when only eight labeled anomalies are available in the training procedure.

Accelerated Execution of Bayesian Neural Networks using a Single Probabilistic Forward Pass and Code Generation
Authors: Bernhard Klein, Falk Selker, Hendrik Borras, Sophie Steger, Franz Pernkopf, Holger Fröning

Abstract
Machine learning models perform well across domains such as diagnostics, weather forecasting, NLP, and autonomous driving, but their limited uncertainty handling restricts use in safety-critical settings. Traditional neural networks often fail to detect out-of-domain (OOD) data and may output confident yet incorrect predictions. Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) address this by providing probabilistic estimates, but incur high computational cost because predictions require sampling weight distributions and multiple forward passes. The Probabilistic Forward Pass (PFP) offers a highly efficient approximation to Stochastic Variational Inference (SVI) by assuming Gaussian-distributed weights and activations, enabling fully analytic uncertainty propagation and replacing sampling with a single deterministic forward pass. We present an end-to-end pipeline for training, compiling, optimizing, and deploying PFP-based BNNs on embedded ARM CPUs. Using the TVM deep learning compiler, we implement a dedicated library of Gaussian-propagating operators for multilayer perceptrons and convolutional neural networks, combined with manual and automated tuning strategies. Ablation studies show that PFP consistently outperforms SVI in computational efficiency, achieving speedups of up to 4200x for small mini-batches. PFP-BNNs match SVI-BNNs on Dirty-MNIST in accuracy, uncertainty estimation, and OOD detection while greatly reducing compute cost. These results highlight the potential of combining Bayesian approximations with code generation to enable efficient BNN deployment on resource-constrained systems.

Abstract
Language-model advice can accelerate reinforcement learning, but every call has a price, and a returned suggestion may be unparsable, unsafe to execute, or no better than the agent's own action. We introduce certified predictive advice gating, a cost-aware controller that forecasts possible advisor responses, values the action actually executed after parsing, execution checks, and cache reuse, and requests advice only when a calibrated lower bound on that value covers the price. Our analysis separates the gap of executed advice, cumulative regret under an intervention-stable fallback, and myopic acquisition loss. On BabyAI GoToObj with Qwen2.5-1.5B and 7B advisors, the gate improves return over no advice by paired $0.029\pm0.016$ and $0.030\pm0.015$ while using more than $97\%$ fewer calls than always asking. Matched-budget comparisons, however, do not resolve an advantage over an early query schedule, so the gain reflects economical advice volume rather than demonstrated state selection. Groupwise calibration raises coverage on positive-value opportunities from $0.47$ to $0.85$, still below the $0.90$ target. A separate headroom-aware gate reports paired gains over an early schedule of $0.032\pm0.012$ and $0.029\pm0.011$ on GoToObj and $0.027\pm0.011$ and $0.028\pm0.012$ on GoToObjDoor, and adds BabyAI and ALFWorld success, decision-stratum coverage, and component timing. These results separate return gains, query placement, and execution screening; finite-sample deployment guarantees remain unestablished.

Follow the Perturbed Leader for Linear Bandits: $\sqrt{T}$ Regret over General Action Sets
Authors: Chen-Yu Wei
First: 2026-10-13T06:42:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:16:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We give the first adversarial linear bandit algorithm based on Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (FTPL) that achieves $\sqrt{T}$ regret over general action sets. Our algorithm attains $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{5/4}\sqrt{T})$ regret using only linear optimization oracles, and the near-optimal $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$ regret when an optimal-design exploration distribution is further available. This improves on the $T^{2/3}$ regret of the FTPL algorithm of Dani and Hayes (2006) for general action sets, and generalizes the $\sqrt{T}$ regret of Lévy et al. (2026) over two special action sets (hypercube and Euclidean ball). Compared to previous oracle-efficient linear bandit algorithms based on continuous exponential weights, the FTPL approach generally has better computational efficiency, and is much simpler to implement. The key algorithmic element is a perturbation distribution that adapts to the past reward estimators. Its simplest form, an adaptive Gaussian perturbation, is straightforward to implement and already attains the regret bound above. We further extend it to two general constructions, random-weighting and elliptical perturbations, each covering distributions from light tails (Gaussian) to heavy tails (Student-$t_2$). On the technical side, we develop a systematic way to analyze the stability term in the FTPL regret through the Fisher information of the perturbation distribution. This builds a general framework for FTPL beyond existing ones such as the hazard rate and the FTRL-FTPL duality.

Abstract
Implementing Security Technical Implementation Guide (STIG) controls remains a demanding operating-system administration task. Each natural-language security requirement must be translated into a precise and persistent configuration change. These changes span Secure Shell (SSH) configuration, authentication policy, audit rules, and kernel parameters. Existing benchmarks do not measure whether a model can perform this task on a live system. Code benchmarks evaluate isolated programs without persistent system state, while agent benchmarks largely focus on applications rather than operating-system configuration. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{HardEval}, an execution-verified benchmark for evaluating language-model-assisted security hardening. Expert-written reference remediations and Open Vulnerability and Assessment Language (OVAL) checks serve as hidden ground truth for grading; they are never shown to the model, and each task states only the required security outcome while withholding the implementation. Evaluation of fourteen frontier and open-weight configurations reveals large and uneven capability differences across security domains. A recurring failure mode is plausible security code that does not satisfy the exact compliance condition. For four open-weight configurations, multi-run Wilson confidence intervals preserve the observed ordering. HardEval is publicly available.

Abstract
Long range wide area network (LoRaWAN) has a long communication range and low power consumption, making it a potential technology for direct-to-satellite (DtS) Internet of Things (IoT) connectivity. However, packet collisions and Doppler impairments brought on by satellite motion can greatly increase the packet loss ratio (PLR) in large-scale DtS-IoT networks. In this paper, we study resource allocation in DtSIoT LoRaWAN by first developing an analytical PLR model that simultaneously takes Doppler impairments and packet collisions in the temporal and frequency domains into account. Furthermore, for 800 EDs, PPO and DQN increase the total number of transmitted packets by 106.7% and 88.0%, respectively, compared with random allocation.

Abstract

Strictly local models enable efficient spatial parallelization but neglect long-range interactions, whereas existing electrostatic extensions introduce global dependencies that hinder distributed computation. By combining long-range physics, electrical-response inference, and distributed computation, this framework extends predictive atomistic modeling toward device-relevant length scales.

A general recipe for data-adaptive anytime-valid envelopes for iterative algorithms
Authors: Soham Bonnerjee, Aaditya Ramdas
First: 2026-10-16T14:52:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T14:52:40+00:00
Comments: 83 pages, 4 figures

Abstract
Most convergence guarantees for iterative stochastic algorithms are stated at a fixed iteration count and do not justify stopping at a data-dependent time. Recent work has constructed anytime-valid envelopes that do, but deterministic envelopes are typically too conservative for practical stopping, and existing data-adaptive ones are derived case-by-case, each coupling a specific algorithm to a specific noise model and boundary construction. This paper provides a general recipe for constructing such anytime-valid data-adaptive boundaries for a large class of iterative algorithms. Performance measures of many of these algorithms satisfy a one-step recursion that reduces the entire error analysis to a single general requirement: time-uniform concentration for predictably weighted sums of noise. We formalize this through a strongly adapted sub-$ψ$ condition, and derive off-the-shelf boundary constructions via continuous and discrete mixtures, stitching, and product betting martingales. We show how these boundaries can be directly coupled with a separate algorithmic analysis that derives the one-step recursion to provide a class of readymade anytime-valid envelopes across diverse settings including stochastic gradient descent (SGD), temporal difference learning, and Bures-Wasserstein SGD. In particular, for SGD, our framework either recovers or significantly sharpens every existing envelope we are aware of. Our constructions come with explicit implementation details and are substantiated by numerical experiments.

Abstract
Learning causal structure from data requires assumptions about mechanisms, noises, observation schemes, and interventions. Historically, causal discovery has relied primarily on conditional independence. This review focuses on modern causal discovery methods that exploit properties of distributions beyond conditional independence. Our organizing principle separates the source of identification from the statistical estimator and its computational implementation. This perspective connects conditional-independence methods with distributional asymmetries, conditional-moment restrictions, and variation across environments or individuals. We examine discovery under latent confounding and feedback, distinguishing causal information about observed variables from reconstruction of latent structure and emphasizing the additional semantics required by cyclic models. We also distinguish population identification, frequentist and Bayesian consistency, uncertainty quantification, and algorithmic guarantees. Throughout, examples show why the same observational distribution can support different conclusions under different model classes. We conclude with guidance for comparing methods at their identifiable targets and a research agenda for uncertainty, heterogeneous populations, non-scalar variables, and the intersection of discrete data, feedback, and hidden confounding.

Abstract
Resampling is the standard remedy for weight degeneracy in particle filters, but in high dimensions it distorts the spectrum of the ensemble covariance in a way that is computable from the importance weights. This paper studies the inverse problem: recovering the pre-resampling spectrum from the observed weights and the empirical spectrum alone, and using it without biasing the filter. We first show that the forward spectral law extends to random importance weights once one conditions on the weights together with the realized offspring counts, rather than on the weights alone. We then construct a regularized free-deconvolution estimator, prove its consistency with an explicit error budget at a fixed resolution scale, and certify model mismatch through a computable forward residual. The repaired spectrum enters the filter only as the preconditioner of a Metropolis-adjusted rejuvenation move, so that spectral error affects mixing efficiency but not the invariant target. For the full filtering cycle, we derive a geometrically summable error recursion under stable scalar dynamics and an explicit law for the cost of abstaining from repair. Pre-registered simulations show that the inversion reduces the spectral error by an order of magnitude, that the corrected preconditioner recovers about 90% of the oracle's sampling efficiency in an anisotropic regime, and that the cost of abstention depends on when abstentions occur rather than on how many. The repair recovers eigenvalue laws, not eigenvectors, and is certified only within an explicitly stated exogenous-weight regime.

Abstract
We introduce an ordinary differential equation (ODE) based deep generative method for learning conditional distributions, named Conditional Föllmer Flow. Starting from a standard Gaussian distribution, the proposed flow could approximate the target conditional distribution very well when the time is close to 1. For effective implementation, we discretize the flow with Euler's method where we estimate the velocity field nonparametrically using a deep neural network. Furthermore, we also establish the convergence result for the Wasserstein-2 distance between the distribution of the learned samples and the target conditional distribution, providing the first comprehensive end-to-end error analysis for conditional distribution learning via ODE flow. Our numerical experiments showcase its effectiveness across a range of scenarios, from standard nonparametric conditional density estimation problems to more intricate challenges involving image data, illustrating its superiority over various existing conditional density estimation methods.

Abstract
The mobility of potential eavesdroppers (Eves) poses a critical challenge to physical-layer security (PLS) in integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) systems, since rapidly varying Eve positions lead to time-varying eavesdropping channels and outdated security decisions. To address this challenge, this paper investigates sensing-driven secure transmission for reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted ISAC systems with a maneuvering Eve. To this end, by exploiting the dual functionality of the ISAC waveform, a multi-view angle estimation method jointly exploiting the base station (BS) and RIS is developed based on MUSIC and maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), enabling three-dimensional (3D) Eve localization, whose accuracy is characterized by the Cramer-Rao lower bound (CRLB). Subsequently, an interacting multiple model extended Kalman filter (IMM-EKF) with out-of-sequence measurement (OOSM) compensation is employed to track and predict the maneuvering Eve. Based on the predicted trajectory, the time-varying eavesdropping channel is updated, and the transmit beamforming and RIS phase shifts are jointly optimized subject to a sensing CRLB constraint. Furthermore, an alternating optimization (AO) algorithm integrating fractional programming, semidefinite relaxation (SDR), and Riemannian manifold optimization is developed to solve the resulting non-convex problem. Finally, numerical results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in accurately tracking maneuvering Eves and enhancing secure transmission in dynamic ISAC environments.

Abstract
Adaptive LLM reinforcement-learning post-training changes multiple training actuators online, including rollout temperature, group size, clipping, KL regularization, verifier allocation, and update budget. Three coupled issues remain unresolved. A future-risk model trained from behavior trajectories need not estimate the risk induced by the controller that will be deployed; a score calibrated on logged state-action pairs can become miscalibrated after selective action choice; and independent per-resource minimum costs do not in general certify a feasible multi-resource continuation. We introduce FSPO, a feedback-state controller for budgeted LLM RL post-training that addresses these issues jointly. FSPO learns a policy-consistent risk-to-go model whose Bellman target follows the same frozen controller used for future decisions, together with a long-horizon utility model. Decision-conditioned trajectory calibration (DCTC) calibrates risk on cross-fitted trajectories generated by actions selected by provisional controllers. A Pareto resource continuation certificate (PRCC) admits an action only when a non-dominated cumulative reservation remains feasible over the residual horizon. Under a matched GRPO resource envelope, FSPO reaches 66.11% held-out and 59.43% OOD accuracy, compared with 64.47% and 57.03% for PB2, the strongest evaluated adaptive baseline. Three paired training seeds give gains of +2.42 and +3.19 percentage points over the contextual bandit on held-out and OOD evaluation. Under high behavior-deployment mismatch, policy-consistent risk lowers selected-decision ECE from 0.108 to 0.053; DCTC lowers it from 0.039 to 0.022 at matched acceptance; PRCC removes false-feasible admissions on an 18-action catalog ($0.197\rightarrow0.000$); and enabling all three components reduces trajectory failure from 0.181 to 0.083 in a factorial ablation.

Abstract
When we say that a robot uses memory, does that memory actually play a role? Success-rate ablations alone cannot establish the use of task-relevant facts: they change information and computation together, while aggregate rates can hide offsetting gains and losses. We propose an Interventional Memory Evaluation Protocol (IMEP) for vision-language-action (VLA) systems to address four questions: (1) Does memory provide a net benefit? Abstract
Traditional methods plan feasible paths for multiple agents in the stochastic environment. However, the methods' iterations with the changes in the environment result in computation complexities, especially for the decentralized agents without a centralized planner. Although reinforcement learning provides a plausible solution because of the generalization for different environments, it struggles with enormous agent-environment interactions in training. Here, we propose a novel centralized training with decentralized execution method based on multi-agent reinforcement learning, which is improved based on the idea of model predictive control.
