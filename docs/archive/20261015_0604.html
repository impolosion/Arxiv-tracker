Cepheid: Decoupling Scale from Structure for Cross-Family Motion Completion
Authors: Chih-Hai Su, Tsung-Wei Ke, Yu-Lun Liu
First: 2026-10-13T17:59:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:59:53+00:00
Comments: 31 pages. Project page: https://su-terry.github.io/cepheid-website/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Sparse-to-dense motion completion recovers dense mesh motion from sparse vertex displacements. Residual priors trained on one geometry family frequently fail to beat parameter-free harmonic extension off-family. Moreover, cross-family training meets a scaling barrier: residual amplitude varies by more than an order of magnitude across geometry families and collapses as observations become denser. Since this scale depends on unknown target motion, it cannot be measured directly at test time. We decouple residual structure from physical scale by learning a dimensionless prior, estimating amplitude from observables and a lightweight correction fitted only on training sources. Our unified model surpasses harmonic extension across all evaluated families, including unseen cloth and legged bodies, without skeletons, registration, or cross-mesh correspondence. The same prior applies without retraining to point-tracked animal video and a robot grasping a soft body. Project page: https://su-terry.github.io/cepheid-website/

Two Clocks: Why Larger Rollout Batches Learn Slower but Fail on Schedule in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards
Authors: Yuante Li, Junwei Quan, Kai Qiu, Kate Zhang, Yicheng Tao
First: 2026-10-13T17:58:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:58:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) relies on costly model-generated responses, motivating larger rollout batches and square-root learning-rate scaling. Reported to approximately preserve progress per sample with one optimizer step per rollout, this rule is less understood in practical multi-step training. We develop a two-clock framework that separates the samples needed to make progress from those consumed before collapse. Under a local quadratic model, the square-root rule preserves first-order drift per sample but not progress: larger batches take fewer, longer updates and lose more to curvature, yielding a learning-rate-dependent critical batch beyond which sample efficiency degrades. A drift-threshold hypothesis predicts a complementary failure clock whose sample count remains approximately invariant along the square-root rule. Controlled GRPO experiments on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B across a $16\times$ batch range support both predictions. Ratio clipping delays failure, especially for larger batches, but the progress gap persists without it. Widening the clip trades stability for a transient speed gain. The progress gap reappears on another dataset, a general-purpose checkpoint, and a 7B model. Finally, we use the two-clock framework to derive a simple multi-step RLVR schedule: start below the critical batch and switch to a larger batch before failure. Our code is available at: https://github.com/WinstonLiyt/two-clocks-rlvr.

MFG: Multi-turn Feedback Guided Molecular Optimization
Authors: Trieu Nguyen, Frazier N. Baker, Ruoxi Gao, Daniel Adu-Ampratwum, Xia Ning
First: 2026-10-13T17:58:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:58:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-objective molecular optimization is a critical process in drug discovery that aims to modify a given molecule to simultaneously improve multiple properties while maintaining a similar overall structure. This process often requires iterative modifications guided by computational or experimental feedback. Recent large language model (LLM)-based methods have begun to explore this iterative approach; however, these methods are not explicitly trained to leverage such feedback, while existing approaches primarily focus on single-turn molecular optimization. To address this gap, we introduce Multi-turn Feedback Guided (MFG) molecular optimization, a reinforcement learning framework that trains an LLM to progressively optimize molecules using feedback from previous turns. MFG introduces a feedback vector capturing both property improvements and source molecule similarity, alongside a feedback-aligned reward function that promotes progressive improvement across turns. We apply MFG over $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O}$, a state-of-the-art LLM model for multi-objective molecular optimization, evaluate $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O-MFG}$ on ten multi-objective molecular optimization tasks. The results demonstrate that $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O-MFG}$ achieves the highest average similarity-constrained success rate ($81.50%$), outperforming the base model $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O}$ ($70.81%$).

Abstract
Learning generalist policies capable of accomplishing a wide range of everyday tasks remains an open challenge in dexterous manipulation. In particular, collecting large-scale manipulation data via real-world teleoperation is expensive and difficult to scale. While learning in simulation provides a feasible alternative, designing multiple task-specific environments and rewards for training is similarly challenging. We propose Dex4D, a framework that instead leverages simulation for learning task-agnostic dexterous skills that can be flexibly recomposed to perform diverse real-world manipulation tasks. Specifically, Dex4D learns a domain-agnostic 3D point track conditioned policy capable of transforming a rigid object from a current pose to a target pose. We train this 'Anypose-to-Anypose' policy in simulation across thousands of objects with diverse pose configurations, covering a broad space of robot-object interactions that can be composed at test time. At deployment, this policy can be zero-shot transferred to real-world tasks without finetuning, simply by prompting it with desired object-centric point tracks extracted from generated videos. During execution, Dex4D uses online point tracking for closed-loop perception and control. Extensive experiments in simulation and on real robots show that our method enables zero-shot deployment for diverse dexterous manipulation tasks and yields consistent improvements over prior baselines. Furthermore, we demonstrate strong generalization to novel objects, scene layouts, backgrounds, and trajectories, highlighting the robustness and scalability of the proposed framework. Abstract
Robots are increasingly autonomous in task execution, yet their improvement remains largely human-driven. This study investigates Physical Recursive Self-Improvement (Physical RSI), in which physical experience guides lasting updates to both a robot's capabilities and its improvement procedure, making subsequent improvement more effective across tasks. Physical RSI faces challenges from noisy observations, continuous dynamics, costly trials, and irreversible consequences. We present an L0-L5 framework that differentiates capability-level from meta-level updates and within-task from across-task gains, reserving L5 for independently verified recursive gain. We study existing methods around harness adaptation and model training, alongside safety and control mechanisms that govern execution and successive updates. Our analysis reveals that, despite substantial progress in self-improvement, the surveyed literature does not yet demonstrate Physical RSI as defined by our framework. We identify research priorities in reliable feedback, causal attribution, safety preservation, and benchmarking, outlining a path toward robots that autonomously improve how they learn while ensuring safety. Abstract
It is difficult to accurately model the physics of the interaction between a robot and the objects it manipulates, including contact mechanics, coupled contact dynamics, deformations, stiffness and friction. We present RoboSimStudio (RSS), an interactive toolkit that makes choices of solver, contact model, and physical parameters explicit and editable, allowing both humans and coding agents to iteratively refine simulations based on observed physical behavior. We implement RSS with the open Newton physics simulation system and describe experiments with human-guided real2sim using (1) visual evaluation, (2) force-feedback and (3) agentic Real2Sim2Real. Abstract

Beyond Single-Model Optimization: Preserving Plasticity in Continual Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Lute Lillo, Nick Cheney
First: 2026-04-16T17:06:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:46:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Continual reinforcement learning must balance retention with adaptation, yet many methods still rely on \emph{single-model preservation}, committing to one evolving policy as the main reusable solution across tasks. Even when a previously successful policy is retained, it may no longer provide a reliable starting point for rapid adaptation after interference, reflecting a form of \emph{loss of plasticity} that single-policy preservation cannot address. Inspired by quality-diversity methods, we introduce \textsc{TeLAPA} (Transfer-Enabled Latent-Aligned Policy Archives), a continual RL framework that organizes behaviorally diverse policy neighborhoods into per-task archives and maintains a shared latent space so that archived policies remain comparable and reusable under non-stationary drift. This perspective shifts continual RL from retaining isolated solutions to maintaining \emph{skill-aligned neighborhoods} with competent and behaviorally related policies that support future relearning. In our MiniGrid CL setting, \textsc{TeLAPA} learns more tasks successfully, recovers competence faster on revisited tasks after interference, and retains higher performance across a sequence of tasks. Our analyses show that source-optimal policies are often not transfer-optimal, even within a local competent neighborhood, and that effective reuse depends on retaining and selecting among multiple nearby alternatives rather than collapsing them to one representative. Together, these results reframe continual RL around reusable and competent policy neighborhoods, providing a route beyond single-model preservation toward more plastic lifelong agents.

Depth Memory
Authors: David González-Martínez, Philipp Nazari, T. Konstantin Rusch, Shiwei Liu
First: 2026-10-13T17:46:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:46:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Residual connections propagate information through a network by additively accumulating layer outputs. Recently proposed alternatives, such as Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) and Attention Residuals (AttnRes), instead use more expressive depth aggregation mechanisms that improve language modeling performance at scale. In this work, we consider the mixing of representations over depth as a problem of storing and retrieving information across layers. Formalizing this perspective, we introduce Depth Memory (DM), a unified framework that describes the standard pre-layer-norm (PreLN) transformer, mHC, and AttnRes in terms of streams that carry representations through the network. One such stream, for example, could be an explicit memory stream that is queried to enrich the input to every layer, and whose output is subsequently written back into the memory for use by later layers. For AttnRes, this memory stream is a growing cache of explicit layer representations. For mHC, it is an expanded residual stream. Building on this view, we propose Linear Depth Memory (LDM), which formulates information retrieval across depth as a regression problem solved using recursive least squares. The resulting architecture propagates three streams across depth: the standard residual stream, a linear associative memory stream, and an inverse covariance stream. For high-throughput training and inference, we propose a hardware-aware implementation of LDM that reduces peak activation memory and memory traffic. LDM consistently achieves the lowest perplexities among PreLN, mHC and AttnRes baselines at the 400M and 1.5B parameter scales, alongside competitive zero-shot accuracy.

First: 2026-10-13T17:46:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:46:25+00:00

Abstract
Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) enables large language models (LLMs) to learn from multi-turn interactions with verifiable outcomes. Textual skills can improve exploration by distilling prior interactions into reusable guidance. However, existing skill-based RL approaches often either assume skill injection is beneficial or rely on indirect feedback or rollout-intensive evaluation to assess its utility. Our pilot studies show that rollout-based utility estimation for skill credit assignment is costly and unstable under limited sampling, while skill benefits vary across candidates and depend on the current policy's competence on each query. To address these challenges, we introduce SkillScaffold, a policy-adaptive training scaffold that combines low-cost skill-utility estimation with selective skill injection. By reusing outcome-verified training trajectories, SkillScaffold generates candidate skills and estimates their utility through outcome-aware likelihood comparisons, without candidate-specific environment rollouts. The resulting utility guides whether and which skill to inject, retaining skill-free execution when no candidate is estimated to help. Joint training enables the Generator to produce skills tailored to the evolving Executor. Scaffolded RL encourages skill internalization for skill-free inference. Experiments on ALFWorld, WebShop, ScienceWorld, and $τ^2$-Bench demonstrate gains over strong agentic-RL and skill-based RL baselines across both short- and long-horizon tasks. Ablations and further analyses support the importance of utility-based selection and abstention. Our code is available at https://github.com/Vaidurya00/SkillScaffold.

Abstract
Off-policy data in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can arise from asynchronous generation, rollout-training mismatch, and response reuse. Token-wise importance weighting avoids multiplying likelihood ratios across a response but generally biases policy gradients. We prove that, under coverage and local expressiveness conditions, full future KL credit makes the exact proximal optimizer the update's unique stationary policy, even for mixtures of older policies, without full-response weights or a fitted critic. With token-wise correction retained, future KL feedback reduces sensitivity to the tested baseline perturbation relative to PPO clipping. A finite future horizon improves AIME24 mean@16 and pass@16 over the full tail, while fixed-policy measurements show that truncation reduces correction variance over the tested horizons.

Abstract

Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure? Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration in LLM Agents
Authors: Ashutosh Hathidara
First: 2026-10-13T16:00:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:00:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Controlling an acting LLM agent means deciding, at each step of a long-horizon task, whether to trust its next action, abstain, or hand off to a human. That decision needs a per-step confidence signal predicting whether the current rollout will eventually succeed. The agent's own self-reported verbalized confidence is the natural candidate, but existing reinforcement learning methods calibrate it against the correctness of a decision at the current step. We propose a method to calibrate it against the eventual outcome of a multi-step episode: under GRPO, the group of rollouts for a task supplies success labels, and a proper scoring rule scores each step's confidence against the realized outcome. In offline RL on recorded trajectories of a Qwen2.5-7B agent in ALFWorld, TextWorld, and WebShop environments, the AUROC of step confidence against episode outcome rises from near chance to 0.68-0.85, and the top-10% of steps the agent is most confident about succeed ~4.3x more often than average. In online RL on the live ALFWorld agent, four calibration rewards improve early-step AUROC, expected calibration error goes from 0.42 to below 0.12, and selective execution improves over both the untrained agent and a format-only RL control, without degrading task success. We also introduce an evaluation protocol that separates episode depth, task difficulty, and attempt-level foresight, which shows where the gain lies: the trained agent recognizes from its first step which tasks it will fail, while telling a succeeding attempt from a failing one at the same task is harder, with some early-window signal emerging on TextWorld.

Does self-distillation improve reasoning?
Authors: Jason Ken Adhinarta, Raina Wu, Ashutosh Sharma, Avinash Balakrishnan, Rameswar Panda, Polina Golland, Yoon Kim, Zhang-Wei Hong
First: 2026-10-13T15:59:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T15:59:36+00:00

Abstract
Self-distillation is an emerging approach that trains a model to imitate its own predictions when conditioned on privileged information, such as correct answers or environmental feedback. Recent methods have reported better sample efficiency and generalization than reinforcement learning (RL), but it remains unclear whether these gains extend beyond in-distribution evaluations and whether they stem from privileged distillation. In this paper, we revisit two representative methods, Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO) and On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), across code, science, tool use, and math reasoning tasks. We find that their reported gains do not consistently generalize to unseen problems and are largely explained by overfitting or transferring existing reasoning behavior, rather than effectively using privileged information. Moreover, self-distillation gradients show weak alignment with policy gradients that directly optimize expected reward, questioning whether they provide an effective direction for policy improvement. Our experiments across Qwen3 models (1.7B-14B) and OLMo-3-7B suggest that effectively leveraging privileged information to improve reasoning remains an open challenge. Our code is available at https://github.com/sleepypandu/does-sd-improve-reasoning

ACCRUE: Continual Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Robot Foundation Models
Authors: Changyeon Kim, Kimin Lee, Yashraj Narang, Yijie Guo
First: 2026-10-13T15:53:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T15:53:26+00:00
Comments: Project website: https://accrue-rl.github.io
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves robot foundation models (RFMs) beyond their demonstrations, but real-world deployment demands more: learning new tasks efficiently while retaining previously acquired behaviors. Existing continual RL methods on top of VLAs mitigate forgetting but rely on on-policy updates with limited forward transfer, whereas off-policy methods improve single-task sample efficiency without addressing learner persistence across tasks. We propose ACCRUE, a continual off-policy RL framework that carries lightweight actor and critic networks across tasks, allowing each stage to reuse behavioral and value knowledge acquired through prior RL. The inherited critic adapts only on current-task transitions and guides current-task actor learning, while prior behavior is preserved by distilling the actor toward cached targets on a small number of retained episodes. Fixed visual features keep the inherited learner and its replay compatible with base-policy updates. Across contact-rich assembly sequences in simulation and on real-robot hardware, ACCRUE accelerates subsequent-task learning with near-zero forgetting and significantly reduces real-robot training time by 31.0\% to 68.5\%.

Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves robot foundation models (RFMs) beyond their demonstrations, but real-world deployment demands more: learning new tasks efficiently while retaining previously acquired behaviors.
Steering the Fidelity in State Preparation on Noisy Bosonic Systems with Risk-Seeking Optimization
Authors: Riccardo Piantoni, Riccardo Pellini, Maurizio Ferrari Dacrema, Paolo Cremonesi
First: 2026-10-13T14:52:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T14:52:00+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In order to execute quantum algorithms on bosonic quantum computers, it is necessary to encode their inputs into a desired target quantum state, with a process called state preparation. In real settings, state preparation methods operate in stochastic scenarios, where the prepared state is represented as a mixed state, that is a probabilistic ensemble of pure states. Therefore, existing state preparation methods optimize a variational ansatz by averaging over all possible sequences of photon loss events contained in the prepared ensemble, yielding an output state which approximates the desired target with a certain average fidelity. Such an approach, however, does not take into account directly the distribution of fidelities across states in the ensemble. In this work, we aim to maximize the fidelity of only a subset of pure states sampled from the prepared ensemble, at the cost of reducing the average fidelity overall. This trade-off is relevant when the goal is to obtain high-fidelity states with a certain probability, rather than maximizing the average fidelity of the prepared ensemble. We borrow and adapt objective functions from risk-sensitive optimization, a framework that optimizes specific characteristics of a distribution rather than only its expectation, and that has been successfully applied in Reinforcement Learning. In the considered experimental settings, our results show that the infidelities of some pure states in the prepared ensemble can be reduced by up to four orders of magnitude when using risk-seeking objectives, compared to those achieved using the standard optimization of the average fidelity. Furthermore, we show that it is possible to control the trade-off between the probability of obtaining high-fidelity states and the lowest infidelity threshold reached, providing the flexibility to select the operating point that best suits a certain application.

ReTerm: Recovery Training for Self-Improving Terminal Agents
Authors: Wei Xie, Kaituo Feng, Shiming Fu, Yong Liu, Xiangyu Yue, Shuangjia Zheng

Abstract
Terminal agents have shown strong abilities in complex coding tasks. However, they often struggle to recover from errors during long-running execution (e.g., command errors, faulty implementations), eventually leading to task failures. To this end, we present ReTerm, a self-improving terminal agent trained by learning how to recover from its own failed terminal tasks. To support its training, we construct a customized data pipeline and produce ReTerm-10K, a high-quality dataset that provides effective recovery demonstrations for SFT cold start. Furthermore, we propose ReTerm-GRPO, an online RL algorithm that forks from the agent's own failure states to generate multiple independent self-recovery attempts. By learning from whether these attempts successfully complete the task, the agent self-improves through its own recovery experience. Built on Qwen3.5-9B, our ReTerm-9B achieves 34.8% Pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, improving over the base model by 14.6 points. Further analysis shows that our method achieves consistently higher recovery rates across diverse error categories compared with standard GRPO training. All code, data and model are released at https://github.com/twothsnd/ReTerm.

Terminal agents have shown strong abilities in complex coding tasks.
Ensemble-MIX: Enhancing Sample-Efficiency in Multi-Agent RL via Selective Ensemble-Based Exploration
Authors: Tom Danino, Nahum Shimkin
First: 2025-06-03T13:13:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T14:29:39+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) methods have achieved state-of-the-art results on a range of multi-agent tasks. Yet, MARL algorithms typically require significantly more environment interactions than their single-agent counterparts to converge, a problem exacerbated by the difficulty in exploring over a large joint action space and the high variance intrinsic to MARL environments. To tackle these issues, we propose a novel algorithm that combines a decomposed centralized critic with decentralized ensemble learning, incorporating several key contributions. The main component in our scheme is a selective exploration method that leverages ensemble kurtosis. We extend the global decomposed critic with a diversity-regularized ensemble of individual critics and utilize its excess kurtosis to guide exploration toward high-uncertainty states and actions. To improve sample efficiency, we train the centralized critic with a novel truncated variation of the TD($λ$) algorithm, enabling efficient off-policy learning with reduced variance. On the actor side, our suggested algorithm adapts the mixed samples approach to MARL, mixing on-policy and off-policy loss functions for training the actors. This approach balances between stability and efficiency and outperforms purely off-policy learning. The evaluation shows our method outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on standard MARL benchmarks, including a variety of SMAC II maps.
Summary / 总结
Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) methods have achieved state-of-the-art results on a range of multi-agent tasks.
Teacher-Guided Tool Learning for Small Language Models via Bidirectional KL Constraints
Authors: Haoxuan Gao, Qin-Wen Luo, Xiang Chen, Yu Tian, Zequn Sun, Xiaofeng Cao, Jiangchao Yao, Sheng-Jun Huang
First: 2026-10-13T14:26:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T14:26:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
External tools can extend the capabilities of small language models (SLMs), enabling their deployment in resource constrained environments. However, their limited capabilities make it difficult for SLMs to discover effective tool use behaviors through reinforcement learning alone, while direct teacher supervision suffers from teacher student behavioral distribution mismatch and may propagate erroneous preferences. To address these challenges, we propose Selective Teacher guided Policy Optimization (SeTPO), which coordinates student exploration with selective teacher guidance and persistent demonstration supervision. Specifically, SeTPO incorporates reverse KL derived token level teacher feedback into policy advantages on low reward trajectories, while auxiliary forward KL supervision preserves teacher demonstrated behaviors throughout training. Under an idealized bidirectional KL objective, we theoretically establish that the optimal policy achieves expected reward no lower than the teacher's while retaining a guaranteed fraction of the teacher's probability mass. Extensive experiments on BFCL v4 and API Bank demonstrate that SeTPO achieves superior overall performance over supervised fine tuning, reinforcement learning, and distillation baselines across all three student scales.
Summary / 总结
External tools can extend the capabilities of small language models (SLMs), enabling their deployment in resource constrained environments.
Geometry-Aware Reinforcement Learning for 2D Irregular Nesting
Authors: Auguste Lehuger, Guillaume Henon-Just
First: 2026-06-09T09:11:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T14:14:04+00:00
Comments: 20 pages, 6 figures, 7 tables. Under review at the Transaction on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)

Abstract
Traditional heuristic solvers for the 2D irregular nesting problem share a fundamental limitation: they are blind to polygon geometry, relying on guided brute-force to navigate the continuous placement space with minimal geometrical guidance. In this paper, we argue that Reinforcement Learning is uniquely positioned to overcome this bottleneck. By pairing an optimization policy with a geometry-aware neural encoder, an agent can automatically discover rich geometric priors directly from data, utilizing these learned intuitions to strategically guide exploration. To realize this, we introduce the Polygons Transformer (PoT), a novel architecture that encodes 2D continuous vector geometries while allowing cross-polygon attention. We couple this novel architecture with a Combinatorial Optimization Reinforcement Learning (CORL) training framework to find optimal solutions. To support this paradigm, we release an open-source training dataset derived from complex geographic contours alongside a dedicated evaluation benchmark. Empirically, our agent slightly exceeds Sparrow, the state-of-the-art heuristic, on small (4-polygon) instances, while a clear scaling gap remains on larger (8-polygon) instances.

Convergence-Rate-Guided Algorithm Design of Expected Improvement for Continuous Simulation Optimization
Authors: Songhao Wang, Jianglin Xia, Haowei Wang, Szu Hui Ng
First: 2026-10-13T14:10:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T14:10:35+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Expected improvement (EI) based on stochastic kriging (SK) surrogate is widely used in continuous simulation optimization, but unknown heteroscedastic noise on simulation output makes three basic design choices consequential: the incumbent used to define improvement, the replication level at each sampled point, and the terminal recommendation. Existing methods typically either assume the noise levels are known or choose these components separately through heuristic rules or empirical calibration. We instead derive a high-probability upper bound on the simple regret and systematically choose the three components within general rule classes to tighten this bound. We first establish a finite-sample confidence bound for the SK predictor with unknown and estimated noise levels as a foundation for the three design choices. We then analyze general posterior-quantile families for both the incumbent and the terminal recommendation. For the incumbent, minimizing the regret bound selects a lower quantile coefficient that matches the coefficient in the SK predictor confidence bound. For the terminal recommendation, minimizing the same bound instead selects the sampled point with the best upper posterior quantile under the same coefficient. These opposite quantile choices reflect the different roles of search and implementation in simulation optimization. Intermediate simulations are exploratory with an optimistic incumbent, while the terminal recommendation is implemented in the real system and is therefore relatively conservative about the remaining posterior uncertainty. Under mild conditions satisfied by common kernels, minimizing a tractable kernel-specific bound yields $Θ(\log T)$ replications. To our knowledge, this is the first simple-regret guarantee for a continuous-domain EI-type simulation optimization method that learns unknown heteroscedastic variances through replications.

Active Inference as a Convex Markov Decision Process
Authors: Nikola Milosevic, Nicolás Hinrichs, Nico Scherf
First: 2026-07-22T13:52:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T13:55:26+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Active Inference (AIF) frames adaptive behavior as the minimization of expected free energy (EFE), combining epistemic and pragmatic objectives within a single variational principle. We frame AIF as policy optimization and show that, for closed-loop control policies, EFE minimization can be formulated as a convex Markov decision process (MDP). This perspective reveals that policy-dependent reward prediction errors transmit natural gradients of the expected free energy backwards in time rather than up a hierarchy. Finally, we show that coupling world-model learning with policy optimization gives active inference the structure of performative reinforcement learning. Together this places EFE minimization within modern reinforcement learning and optimization theory and opens a route toward principled algorithms for active inference.

Selective Successor Eligibility Traces for RLVR
Authors: Chaoli Mou, Xinning Chen, Zhan Zhuang, Qinghe Zheng, Deyuan Kong, Yu Zhang
First: 2026-05-07T10:11:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T13:21:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has become a key approach for improving the reasoning abilities of large language models. However, widely used critic-free algorithms such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) suffer from coarse-grained temporal credit assignment, as they assign the same trajectory-level advantage to all tokens and fail to distinguish the contributions of different reasoning steps. To address this challenge, we propose Selective Successor Eligibility Traces (S-trace), an critic-free eligibility trace method that enables more effective credit assignment. S-trace introduces successor importance reweighting to recover downstream credit signals under PPO-style clipping and propagates them to selected historical tokens through entropy-based token selection. Theoretically, we establish an upper bound on its discrepancy from the raw eligibility traces-based policy gradient estimator, jointly controlled by the KL divergence and the trace decay factor $λ$. Experiments show that S-trace achieves absolute gains in average pass@16 of 1.80\% and 6.17\% over GRPO, and 2.32\% and 0.87\% over trace-style GRPO($λ$), on Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B, respectively, while maintaining robust gains on Qwen3-8B and producing shorter responses.

Abstract
Modern communication systems impose strict demands on data rate, reliability, and power efficiency. In this context, emerging communication paradigms such as identification via channels have become an important topic in post-Shannon information theory, offering the potential for substantially higher identification rates than in conventional channel coding.Deterministic identification is particularly interesting for specialized communication scenarios because it provides a balance between implementation complexity and the communication gains due to higher identification rates. It is therefore a promising communication scheme for future communication systems, including molecular communication systems. Additive Gaussian channels, particularly the additive white Gaussian channel, are among the most important channel models for analyzing the performance of communication systems in information and communication theory. This importance stems from both their mathematical tractability and their ubiquitous appearance in practical applications. To date the deterministic identification capacity for additive Gaussian channels remains unknown even for the simplest case of the additive white Gaussian channel. In this paper, we establish tight bounds on the deterministic identification capacity of additive Gaussian channels by introducing a new perspective on deterministic identification. To this end, we apply results from lattice theory to obtain new capacity results.
