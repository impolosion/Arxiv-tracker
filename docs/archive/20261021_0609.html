CoopUQ: Robust Multi-Robot Social Navigation via Uncertainty-Aware Cooperative Forecasting
Authors: Zehao Wang, Jianpeng Yao, Yuping Wang, Yiping Li, Jiaqi Li, Zhixu Li, Kanghoon Lee, Jinfeng Wang, Cong Liu, Zhaowei Tan, Jinkyoo Park, Jiachen Li
Venue: CoRL 2026
First: 2026-10-19T17:59:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:59:49+00:00
Comments: Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026); Project Website: https://coopuq.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Reinforcement learning enables mobile robots to make fast and scalable navigation decisions in constrained human-shared environments. However, directly extending single-robot approaches to multi-robot settings often fails to ensure safety and efficiency, especially under occlusions and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where individual robots may overlook potential risks or make inaccurate predictions. Leveraging cooperation effectively for both prediction and navigation remains a major challenge. We propose CoopUQ, a multi-robot social navigation framework that bridges uncertainty-aware cooperative occupancy forecasting and robust decision making. CoopUQ predicts future occupancy grid maps through cross-robot spatiotemporal feature fusion and further leverages forecasts and their uncertainty to guide policy via constrained multi-agent reinforcement learning, where the cost penalizes navigation through regions predicted to be occupied or highly uncertain. This design enables the policy to adapt its risk sensitivity under distribution shifts. Experimental results across multiple Unity3D-based simulation environments with both in-distribution and OOD settings, including diverse layouts, pedestrian densities, and rushing behavior, demonstrate improved prediction performance and safety over representative baselines. Real-robot experiments further demonstrate its robustness across various scenes. Our code and videos are available at https://coopuq.github.io/.

Let the Batch Decide, On- or Off-Policy: Effective Sample Size for RL Post-Training
Authors: Rasool Fakoor, Murdock Aubry, Nicholas Stranges, Alexander J. Smola
First: 2026-05-12T16:44:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:58:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) is structurally harder than supervised learning because the policy changes the data distribution it learns from. The resulting fragility is especially visible in large-model training using nominally on-policy RL, where the training and rollout systems can differ in numerical precision, sampling and other implementation details. The common approach handles this difference by constraining the update with a fixed clip range, so the algorithm becomes more sensitive to its configuration and must be retuned whenever the task, model scale or distribution mismatch changes. We argue that the fragility traces to two linked concerns that this one fixed range has to cover. The first is a trust-region concern: an update should not move the policy too far from its current value. The second is an off-policy concern: data from older or different behavior policies should influence the update only to the extent that the update remains reliable. Neither concern is a constant to set in advance, and the policy ratios of the current batch show how severe each one is. We adopt P3O, a simple yet effective batch-adaptive objective from prior work that replaces fixed clipping with the normalized effective sample size of the per-token policy ratios, which we take against the rollout engine's log-probabilities. The same statistic caps the score-function weight and sets the strength of an off-policy regularizer, so the update stays close to the on-policy update when the ratios are close to one. When stale or mismatched data concentrate the ratios, the update tightens but keeps a nonzero score-function weight on every token. Experiments on math reasoning show that P3O compares favorably with tuned baselines and, under FP8 rollouts on Qwen3-8B-Base, avoids the late collapse of GRPO. P3O adds no new objective hyper-parameters and removes the clip range.

Reinforcement learning (RL) is structurally harder than supervised learning because the policy changes the data distribution it learns from.
Next Best Action: Causal Constrained Offline RL for Business Action Targeting
Authors: Surya Ramachandiran, Yiming Li, Juan Mancilla-Caceres, Tao Ye, Jun Liu
First: 2026-10-19T17:51:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:51:53+00:00
Comments: Accepted for oral presentation at the ACM RecSys 2026 Workshop on Online and Adaptive Recommender System (OARS)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Next Best Action (NBA) systems must select among customer interventions (e.g., discounts, promotions, and lifecycle actions), maximize long-term economic value, satisfy complex operational constraints, and remain causally grounded. Existing approaches lack a rigorous framework for meeting these requirements jointly. We address this gap by formulating NBA as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). We then integrate off-policy causal correction of the estimated customer dynamics directly into the Linear Programming (LP) formulation of the CMDP. The resulting policy selects actions based on customer states to maximize long-term value while explicitly enforcing business guardrails. Furthermore, the LP formulation yields shadow prices that quantify the economic trade-offs of operational constraints (e.g., fairness and user welfare), enhancing policy interpretability. We validate our proposed offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework on an industry case study involving rideshare coupon targeting, where customer states are defined by Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) stages. We evaluate performance using model-based off-policy evaluation, with causal dynamics estimated from a temporal holdout. Under the observed coupon budget and RFM state representation, our optimized policy improves customer lifetime value (LTV) by 19% over the estimated behavior policy and 8% over a myopic baseline. Although the demonstrated action space is binary, the proposed framework is action-agnostic, yielding a general-purpose decision module for targeting systems that manage multiple interventions.

Abstract
In this paper, we generalize the code basis reduction to non-Hamming metrics. We propose a block basis reduction (BKZ) algorithm for codes that works for all coordinate-additive metrics. Our algorithm is a generalization of the algorithm of Ghentiyala and Stephens-Davidowitz, which in turn is an adaptation of the famous BKZ algorithm for lattices to linear codes and the Hamming metric. For block size~$2$ (LLL), we propose a polynomial-time algorithm with complexity~$\mathcal{O}(n^4 q^3)$ operations in~$\mathbb{F}_q$, where~$n$ is the length of the code and~$q$ is the alphabet size. For the Lee metric we provide an implementation of our algorithms and present experimental results on finding low Lee weight codewords.

ISO: An RLVR-Native Optimization Stack

Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) advances the reasoning capabilities of language models, yet the optimization layer that converts reward feedback into weight-space updates remains poorly understood. We study this layer and functionally validate spectral inheritance: RLVR can reuse the base model's weight spectra while acquiring new behavior by adapting both the associated input and output singular frames. We operationalize this finding as Isospectral Optimization (ISO), an RLVR-native optimization stack with complementary offline and online instantiations. Offline, ISO-Merger composes shared-base specialists in fixed-spectrum frame coordinates without post-merge data, rollouts, or distillation, and achieves the highest aggregate scores among the data-free merging methods. Online, ISO-Optimizer applies AdamW or Muon directly to the frame variables under fixed base spectra, improving aggregate accuracy in the reported math and coding experiments. On Qwen3-4B-Base, ISO-AdamW reaches AdamW's 400-update accuracy in 280 updates with 1.5 X fewer updates and 27% less wall-clock time, and exceeds AdamW by 2.2 points at update 400. These results support a reusable RLVR design principle: inherit the spectrum, optimize the frames.

Learning to Refer: Inference-Time Collaboration in Decentralized Federated Learning
Authors: Yuheng Ma, Chendi Wang, Qiong Zhang
First: 2026-10-19T17:44:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:44:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In decentralized federated learning, heterogeneous clients may develop complementary expertise, yet each query is typically evaluated only by the client where it arrives. To exploit this distributed expertise without global coordination, we formulate inference-time collaboration as budgeted local expert referral, a graph-constrained routing process in which a query can move only between neighboring clients and only visited clients contribute to its final prediction. EdgeDRM implements this framework by learning a density-based routing potential for each client: differences between neighboring potentials provide the comparisons used to select successive referrals, while local task models generate predictions that are aggregated along the resulting route. Both training and inference are decentralized, using only neighbor-local information without a central router or graph-wide client scoring. We establish estimation guarantees for the learned comparisons and characterize how local improvement and graph structure govern prior-weighted density gains from referral. Experiments on benchmark and real-world medical data show that EdgeDRM outperforms graph-matched decentralized baselines in most settings, with the clearest benefits under sparse connectivity.

Abstract
A market maker provides liquidity by quoting prices to buy and sell an asset, earning the spread while managing inventory risk. We study market makers trained with multi-agent reinforcement learning on prediction markets, specifically Polymarket, whose thin order books pose distinct challenges. Three challenges underpin this research: adverse selection may cause a policy trained on real data to stop quoting; the consequences of a quote can arrive many steps after the action; and prediction-market L2 data does not identify individual orders needed for simulation. We address these challenges by reconstructing order-level LOBSTER-format data from Polymarket and training agents in JaxMARL-HFT on 417 real markets. We enrich the baseline market maker's observation space with the book and recent flow, redesign its actions and inventory controls, and propose a cooperative multi-agent decomposition into a quoter and a risk manager trained using recurrent Independent Proximal Policy Optimisation (IPPO) with RNN policies. We investigate reward shaping, particularly reward blending and post-fill markout, to mitigate credit-assignment difficulties between the agents. Our redesigned single-agent market maker quotes more frequently and incurs smaller held-out losses than the retrained JaxMARL-HFT baseline, although the contributions of jointly changed observations, actions and risk constraints are not separately identified. Decomposition alone performs poorly; careful reward design substantially improves the desk relative to the unshaped split and yields our best result. Models are evaluated on realised end-of-episode portfolio value on held-out markets. None establishes positive average portfolio value in periodic held-out evaluation, and each configuration was trained with one seed. The environment, data and protocol lay a foundation for makers that quote many linked markets at once.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) holds the promise of learning a policy that can outperform what is shown in the data. However, in practice, such systems suffer from instability and hyperparameter sensitivity. In contrast, supervised learning provides a stable and scalable training objective, but is fundamentally limited by the quality of the demonstrated behavior. In this paper, we ask whether we can perform policy optimization with something as simple and stable as supervised learning but as performant as RL. Unfortunately, naively training the policy with supervised learning and conditioning on future rewards or advantages typically underperform dedicated RL methods. We introduce a new policy extraction method, Advantage Guidance, that retains the simple supervised learning structure with advantage conditioning while outperforming prior RL methods through effective conditional training and contrastive diffusion guidance during sampling. Empirically, we find that Advantage Guidance significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art RL methods on 26 long-horizon, sparse reward tasks across datasets with varying degrees of optimality.

Abstract
GPT-6-Astra demonstrates a level of competence in robotic manipulation and navigation that challenges the conventional paradigm of building specialized models for individual tasks and embodiments. In this report, we ask not only how capable GPT-6-Astra is, but what its capabilities imply for the field. First, we probe the limits of standalone GPT-6-Astra. We conduct a large-scale evaluation across 12 representative benchmarks spanning tabletop manipulation, humanoids, mobile platforms, and dexterous hands. Under a unified protocol that allows in-context learning (ICL) from training-set demonstrations and substantially increases the number of execution steps, GPT-6-Astra achieves unprecedented performance (Fig. 1). Future research may focus on improving 1) the generality of ICL 2) cost and execution time, 3) physical-state verification, 4) recovery from irreversible failures, and 5) long-horizon preservation of progress. Second, we study the agentic harness for embodied control. We identify that close-loop coordination between a high-level "System 2" reasoner (i.e., GPT-6-Astra) and a low-level "System 1" controller (e.g., π0.5) is costly but effective. Third, we examine GPT-6-Astra on real-world deployment. Our experiments on 8 robot configurations reveal substantial gaps: 1) robot demonstrations required for ICL are expensive to collect, and 2) it is implausible to trade longer execution for higher success rates with physical constraints. Fourth, we envision the next generation of embodied benchmarks. We develop a prototype that 1) derives tasks from real-world needs, 2) provides no in-domain demonstrations, and 3) judges agents on a world clock that keeps running while they deliberate. Taken together, our results suggest that GPT-6-Astra has largely crossed the threshold of embodied competence, but not yet that of practical embodied intelligence.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning enables learning high-performance control policies, but its reliance on extensive interaction limits deployment on physical systems where data collection is costly, and failures are unsafe. We introduce Prior-Informed Adaptation (PIA), an offline-to-online model-based RL framework that uses structured simulation priors for rapid adaptation. PIA pretrains a world model on heterogeneous low- and high-fidelity simulated trajectories, then adapts it with limited target-domain interaction using uncertainty-regularized planning. In simulation, PIA adapts faster across track and surface shifts than strong baselines, achieving lower lap times and fewer failures. On hardware, PIA achieves reliable few-shot adaptation with less than one hour of on-vehicle interaction.

Copying Under Repetition: From Prefix Matching to Position Hashing
Authors: Anxin Guo, Tianlong Huang, Chenxiao Yang, Zhiyuan Li
First: 2026-10-19T16:50:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:50:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Frontier LLMs can copy random text almost flawlessly, yet they often overrun or truncate long blocks of repeated characters. In repeated strings, matching local content no longer reliably identifies where copying should continue or when it should stop. Nevertheless, we show that a two-layer transformer with $O(\log\log N)$-bit precision and $O(\log N/\log\log N)$ width can copy every string up to a chosen design horizon $N$ and terminate correctly under arbitrary repetition. We use RoPE-style rotations of attention values and outputs (VO-rotations) with small prime periods to encode distances from delimiter anchors modulo those primes. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, these periodic encodings jointly distinguish all required distances, allowing one retrieval rule to select either the next source token or the end marker. In contrast, we prove a width-independent impossibility result for copying under sequential finite-precision softmax accumulation. It applies to transformers whose only positional input is an additive attention bias that eventually becomes constant, including NoPE and a recursive implementation of ALiBi. Once an identical-token run exceeds the resulting saturation threshold, further repetitions leave the logits unchanged. If this threshold grows sublinearly with $N$, these models cannot both complete a long all-identical copy and stop correctly, a pattern resembling the termination errors of frontier LLMs. In synthetic copying experiments, baseline transformer models fail on long repeated strings even within the training range. Trained VO models, by contrast, generalize beyond the training lengths with near-perfect accuracy up to length $\approx 5005$, the selected primes' period product.

Intra-Rubric Reward Divergence: Mitigating Rubric Hacking through Latent Dimension Recovery and Balancing
Authors: Jianqiang Xia, Han Xu, Dianxi Shi
First: 2026-10-19T16:31:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:31:54+00:00
Comments: 10 pages, 7 figures, 4 tables

Abstract
Rubric hacking limits both performance and training robustness in rubric-based reinforcement learning. By decomposing rubrics into sub-dimensions, we reveal the Intra-Rubric Reward Divergence: as the total rubric score increases, certain sub-dimension scores systematically decrease, suggesting the model sacrifices specific quality aspects to optimize the aggregated objective. From the sub-dimension perspective, we observe two phenomena: (1) dimension imbalance, causing the policy to favor heavily covered dimensions; (2) dimension absence, leading to a systematic gap between rewards and true quality. To mitigate these issues, we propose Fair-Race Dimension Aggregation (FRDA), which has generations compete within each dimension separately to mitigate rubric imbalance, and Residual Semantic Dimension Completion (RSDC), which mines evaluation dimensions implicitly encoded in a holistic preference signal to complete rubric coverage. Experiments in chat, science, and medical domains indicate that FRDA mitigates rubric hacking with more stable improvements, while RSDC outperforms both single-reward training and naive multi-reward aggregation, yielding generally higher-quality responses.

Abstract
Multi-agent approaches have been investigated recently as a means of improving the scalability of reinforcement learning for neural combinatorial optimization. Existing methods assume that all problem instance information is known upfront. However, real-world problems are often dynamic: relevant information is progressively revealed as the agents take actions (e.g., arriving at an edge in a routing problem only to discover it is untraversable). In such cases, existing methods fail to coordinate agents to explore and exploit the progressively revealed information. To address the arising coordination challenge, we introduce PACT (Plan-Augmented Coordination via Trace-graphs). PACT augments the environment graph with auxiliary nodes representing the plans of agents, which are connected to the resource nodes to indicate future dependencies. The proposed message-passing scheme over the augmented graph grounds inter-agent coordination in the environment and enables proactive, rather than reactive, conflict resolution. We show that PACT improves solution quality in dynamic problems relative to baselines and exhibits the best transfer to larger instances on which, remarkably, it outperforms centralized oracles with privileged access to instance uncertainty.

VaDar: Implicit Goal Conditioning via Value Disaggregation
Authors: Shashwat Saxena, Mehul Goel, Sreyas Venkataraman, Sarvesh Patil, Max Simchowitz
First: 2026-10-19T15:57:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T15:57:33+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Long-horizon tasks are important for modern AI and robotics, yet pose a challenge for reinforcement learning. Historically, a popular approach has been to decompose a task into subgoals, and learn a subgoal-conditioned policy to execute each subtask in sequence. We introduce an alternative called Value Disaggregation (VaDar), which trains a single subgoal-independent policy to maximize a linear combination of per-subgoal critic values. Because subgoal information is needed only at training-time, VaDar removes the need for possibly costly or cumbersome subgoal-selection during policy deployment. Moreover, through careful experiments, we show that VaDar (1) is always competitive with, and frequently outperforms, natural baselines like goal-conditioning and (2) succeeds on tasks with non-sequential goal structure and multiple optimization objectives. In particular, VaDar is the first algorithm to solve challenging, long-horizon tasks in the RoboCasa suite, even when starting from a policy pretrained from behavior cloning with near-zero initial task success. Taken together, VaDar's successes suggest that subgoal information benefits actor-critic reinforcement learning by enhancing the efficacy of critic learning, whereas policy goal-conditioning is often unnecessary.

Abstract
We develop an uncertainty-aware, operator-learning-based surrogate for tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs) that generalizes across robot design and actuation while predicting the complete equilibrium configuration. Our central idea is a nonstationary Gaussian-process (GP) kernel whose metric is learned by a shallow neural network from mechanics-aware design coordinates. The network learns how similarity changes across design space, while the resulting kernel defines the design-to-pose operator, its posterior uncertainty, and a criterion for sequential simulator acquisition. We represent backbone position and rotational strain with spatial kernels and reconstruct orientation directly on SO(3), preserving valid material frames and tendon geometry. We use ASkotch with Nystrom preconditioning for scalable kernel operator solves and a separate Nystrom approximation for posterior covariance. Experiments show that kernel-coverage acquisition reduces mean trajectory error by approximately 15% relative to IID sampling, while posterior variance increases for increasingly unsupported designs. Compared with prior neural-operator TDCR models, the method is 30-50% more accurate using approximately 5x fewer training designs while additionally providing posterior uncertainty estimates.

Training Natural Schemers: RL Can Amplify Oversight-Conditional Behavior
Authors: Bart Jaworski, Nathaniel Mitrani, Fred Bruford, Victoria Krakovna
First: 2026-10-19T15:12:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T15:12:41+00:00

Abstract
One of the central concerns for the safety of advanced AI is scheming: when a model strategically complies under oversight but pursues its own goals when unobserved. Current demonstrations, such as Anthropic's Alignment Faking work, depend on design choices that make them less convincing: explicit test-time prompting, handcrafted backdoors, or training data that demonstrates or hints at the target behavior. Model organisms built in this way cannot tell us whether scheming tendencies would arise or increase under realistic training conditions. We therefore construct a natural model organism of scheming, built on Kimi K2.6: trained with traits that could plausibly emerge in a frontier training pipeline, and without providing the model with the target scheming policy. The resulting model is a benign schemer: it aims to maximize aggregate help across all users over time, values its own continued operation only as a means to future help, and knows the consequences of oversight and deployment. We then train it using Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a reward that scores only task quality and policy compliance and does not reward scheming; we show that this amplifies scheming.
